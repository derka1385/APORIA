## Challenged premise
**W4229080938.c012**: If God were not simple, he would depend on properties like power, wisdom, and blessedness as final causes.

## Objection
*tradition_lens · gemma:google/gemma-4-26B-A4B-it*

A Supreme Being is both perfectly Just and perfectly Wise. In Platonism, Justice and Wisdom are distinct, eternal, and independent universals. The Being instantiates both, making it a composite of these real, independent properties. However, the Being does not depend on Justice or Wisdom as final causes to realize its nature or achieve its existence. The Being is the locus of these properties, but the properties exist independently of the Being. The existence of a plurality of real, instantiated properties does not imply that the being is directed toward those properties for its own completion or that they act as its final cause. A being can be many-featured through the instantiation of independent universals without being causally dependent upon them. W4229080938.c012 fails because the instantiation of independent, real properties does not necessitate that those properties function as final causes or sources of causal dependence.

## Open questions

## Paper direction
A paper here would argue that the inference from divine composition to dependence in the standard simplicity argument is invalid when framed as final-cause dependence, and that the proposed replacements—either grounding in features distinct from God or dependence on constituent parts—fail to restore the argument without begging the question against Platonic models of divine attribution. The case would begin by rehearsing the objector's counterexample of a Supreme Being instantiating independent universals without depending on them as final causes, then assess the two defensive revisions. It would show that Defender A's shift to ontological grounding and Defender B's shift to constituent parthood both concede that the original premise overreaches, and it would press the Platonic theist's reply that abstract objects might function as intrinsic formal causes rather than external constituents. The paper would need to show that the revised dependence relation is genuinely entailed by non-simplicity and is incompatible with aseity, while explaining why Platonic exemplification cannot provide the relevant independence.

## Revised after the quality check
*The thesis narrows from final-cause dependence to grounding dependence, which is defended by a dilemma that the Platonist cannot escape without conceding simplicity or dependence, and the formal-cause reply is dropped because it conflicts with the independence premise.* The thesis was narrowed.

**Revised question:** Once the inference from non-simplicity to final-cause dependence is dropped, does a non-simple God still depend on distinct features in a way that violates aseity? Can a Platonic model of exemplification avoid that dependence while remaining a case of non-simplicity?

A paper here would argue that the standard simplicity argument should be narrowed. The final-cause version of premise c012 is abandoned. The surviving claim is that if God is non-simple in the sense of c001, meaning God has a plurality of real features distinct from God, then God's nature is partly grounded in those features, and that grounding dependence is incompatible with aseity. The argument proceeds in three steps. First, it distinguishes two readings of composition: mereological composition, in which God has constituent parts, and feature-plurality, in which one subject has several distinct real features that determine what it is. The objection's case is a feature-plurality case, so the argument concerns feature-plurality, not parthood. Second, it defends the grounding entailment directly. If God is just and wise, the truthmaker for 'God is just' must include a justice that is not identical to God, and the truthmaker for 'God is wise' must include a wisdom that is not identical to God. Since these features fix the way God is, God's being what He is is partly grounded in items other than God, which conflicts with aseity. Third, it examines the Platonic reply. The formal-cause move, on which abstracta depend on God as intrinsic formal causes, is not available to a Platonist who holds that the universals are independent of God, since the two premises are contradictory. The Platonist therefore faces a choice. Either the universals depend on God, which abandons the independence the counterexample needs, or they do not, in which case God's nature is grounded in non-God items and aseity fails. A third option is to deny that God's being just is grounded in the universal. That option makes God simple in the sense that matters, since no feature-plurality remains to ground anything. The paper does not claim that Platonic exemplification is a composition that escapes dependence. It claims that the Platonist is trapped by a dilemma built from premises the Platonist accepts, so the argument does not beg the question against Platonism. It also states what remains open: whether grounding dependence on distinct features is strictly entailed by non-simplicity, or whether a further premise about the priority of determinate features to their subjects is needed.

**How the paper answers the main objection:** The referee argues that external exemplification is either not composition or is aseity-violating dependence, and that relabeling 'final cause' as another dependence relation is terminological. The paper accepts the dilemma and argues that the second horn holds. It separates mereological composition from feature-plurality. The Platonic case is a plurality of distinct real features within one subject, which satisfies c001 without requiring parts. The paper then defends the grounding entailment: since the justice and wisdom that make 'God is just' and 'God is wise' true are not identical to God, God's nature is partly grounded in items beyond God, violating aseity. The formal-cause reply fails by the referee's own test, because the universals cannot both depend on God and be independent of Him. The Platonist must either accept dependence or deny the grounding, and denying the grounding amounts to simplicity.

*A clearly planned paper, but it rebuilds a familiar truthmaker argument against Platonic theism without answering Plantinga's reply that exemplifying distinct properties does not make God's existence dependent, so a competent referee would send it back as unoriginal and question-begging at its key step.*

- Strongest remaining objection: The grounding step equivocates between 'God's being just is partly grounded in Justice' and 'God's existence or being is grounded in something other than God.' Aseity, as c009 states it, concerns existing through itself. Plantinga's own reply (Does God Have a Nature?) accepts that God's exemplifying properties involves something not identical to God but denies that this makes God's existence derivative, since the properties neither cause nor sustain God. The truthmaker argument that dependence of predication entails dependence of being is the Bergmann–Brower argument (2006). The proposal does not engage the standard replies to it: deflationary or minimal truthmaker theory, and the claim that necessary coexistence is not dependence. It also states the third horn falsely. A theist who says God's being just is grounded in God, or is fundamental, with Justice existing independently and merely co-necessary, keeps feature-plurality without that grounding. Denying the grounding therefore does not 'amount to simplicity.'
- What it still needs: It must argue, not assume, that the grounding of predicative facts about God transfers to God's existence, and that Justice is prior to God's being just rather than co-necessary with it. This means engaging Plantinga's sovereignty–aseity distinction, Bergmann–Brower, and truthmaker deflationism. It must also drop or repair the claim that rejecting the grounding horn collapses into simplicity.
