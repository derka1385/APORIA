## Objection

Consider City X, whose existence is grounded in two distinct facts. First, the concrete arrangement of streets, buildings, and inhabitants is a brute fact that makes the proposition 'City X exists' true in virtue of its own spatial configuration. Second, a sovereign's charter legally creates and sustains the city; the charter is a separate fact that also truth‑makes the same proposition. Truthmaker theory permits multiple, co‑existent truthmakers for a single truth without contradiction, because each truthmaker provides a different explanatory basis. Thus, a thing can exist both through a self‑grounded fact and through another fact without incoherence.

## Open questions

## Paper direction

The case would develop the City X example to show that without an independent criterion for distinguishing genuinely self-grounding existence from merely partial, brute-fact grounding, the revised premise either remains open to counterexamples or becomes an ad hoc restriction tailored only to God. The strongest reply to anticipate is that City X is disanalogous: God is a necessary being whose essence is identical to existence, whereas a city is a contingent aggregate sustained by an extrinsic legal fact, and truthmaker plurality concerns propositional truth rather than metaphysical constitution. To succeed, the paper would need to show that these disanalogies cannot be established without already assuming that God is simple, and that the very notion of 'wholly' self-grounded existence collapses the distinction between divine aseity and creaturely derivative existence in a way that undermines the argument's original force.
