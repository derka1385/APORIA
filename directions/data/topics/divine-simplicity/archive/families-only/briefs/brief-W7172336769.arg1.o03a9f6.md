## Challenged premise
**W7172336769.c009**: To address the critics’ underlying concerns, Fregean diagnostics must be located within the broader semantic‑metaphysical framework of the doctrine of analogy.

## Objection
*blind_thought_experimenter · anthropic:sonnet*

Consider Ruth, a seminary teacher who must grade two essays. Essay A says 'God is wise' and Essay B says 'God is wise in the way a clever chess player is wise.' Ruth applies the framework of res significata and modus significandi: she asks whether the perfection signified (wisdom) is ascribed primarily to God, with creatures possessing it only derivatively. Her rubric instructs her to reward A and penalize B. But when a student, Tomas, asks what 'primarily' means and how the ratio of proportionality between God and creatures is fixed, Ruth can only point to the doctrine's own vocabulary. Her colleague Nadia, a Fregean, asks which sense of 'wise' the rubric fixes, and gets no answer that distinguishes the two essays by truth-conditions. Both teachers agree on grades yet cannot say what each grade tracks. The framework generates verdicts in practice while the semantic content that would ground them remains unspecified, so the claim that analogy is the place where Fregean diagnostics are located does not follow: the analogical framework is invoked to license judgments it does not itself determine.

## Strongest responses and why they failed
- **Defender A**: Distinguishes the determinacy of admissibility from the determinacy of truth-conditional sense, arguing that the res significata/modus significandi distinction supplies a principled asymmetry that constrains which predications are permissible even if it does not yield a Fregean sense. — *It conceded that the argument overstates determinacy because the metaphysical grounding fixes reference only if the participation relation is itself specified, and the argument does not specify it in a way that yields truth-conditions distinguishing the predications, so the framework licenses practice without supplying the Fregean-style content the objection demands.*
- **Defender B**: Relocates determinacy from the semantic vocabulary to the metaphysics of participation, claiming that intrinsic perfection predication states what God is in Godself and that Essay B falsely imposes a creaturely mode, with the modus significandi determined by this metaphysics supplying the requisite sense. — *It conceded that ontological determination is not operational determination; the positive sense of the predicate remains specified only by negation and metaphysical participation rather than by a Fregean sense that a practitioner can independently articulate, so the argument conflates metaphysical truth-making with practical criterion-giving.*

## Open questions
- Can the metaphysics of participation be specified in a non-circular manner that yields truth-conditions for analogical predications without presupposing the very distinctions they are meant to ground?
- Does the res significata/modus significandi distinction provide more than a negative formal rule, and if not, can a purely negative criterion suffice for the determinacy required by theological practice?
- Is the gap between metaphysical truth-making and operational criterion-giving a general feature of analogical theories of religious language or specific to the Thomistic-Fregean synthesis?
- If the positive ratio of proportionality must remain semantically open, what distinguishes analogical predication from a sophisticated agnosticism about the divine nature?

## Paper direction
A paper here would argue that the Thomistic doctrine of analogy, even when grounded in a metaphysics of participation, fails to supply the operational criteria needed to distinguish correct from incorrect divine predications and is therefore limited to negative constraints that leave the positive content of theological language undetermined. The case would centre on the gap between metaphysical truth-making and practical criterion-giving exposed by the Ruth and Nadia thought experiment, showing that the res significata/modus significandi distinction yields only a formal rule to exclude creaturely modes without fixing the positive sense of analogical predicates. The strongest reply to anticipate is that the semantic and metaphysical are inseparable, so the metaphysics of participation fixes truth-conditions by identifying intrinsic perfection predication with God’s simple essence. The paper would need to show that this reply conflates ontological determination with operational determination, and that unless the ratio of proportionality can be specified in a way accessible to a competent practitioner who does not already possess the correct theological answer, the doctrine cannot deliver the determinate content it promises while retaining semantic openness.

## Revised after the quality check
*The revision retargets the paper from the necessity premise c009 to the sufficiency and determinacy claims at c042 and c005, replaces the undefined 'operational determinacy' standard with one internal to the doctrine, and narrows the thesis to where remotion and eminence stop supplying determinate content.* The thesis was narrowed.

**Revised question:** Given that the Thomistic doctrine of analogy is committed to the claim that God is not known quidditatively, what standard of determinacy must theological predication meet for theological practice to be meaningful, and does the doctrine's account of predication (remotion, causality, eminence, and the res significata/modus significandi distinction) meet that standard?

A paper here would argue that the Thomistic doctrine of analogy, taken on its own terms, supplies enough determinacy to ground the discrimination of admissible from inadmissible divine predications, but that this determinacy is limited in a specific and identifiable way. The paper would not argue that analogy fails because it does not yield operational criteria usable without a prior grasp of the divine nature, since Thomism rejects that demand by design. The thesis is narrower: the sufficiency claim at c042 and c005 depends on a standard of determinacy that the doctrine can meet only partly. The paper would first state a non-question-begging standard drawn from the doctrine itself. Practical determinacy is met when (i) the perfection signified by a predicate is fixed by a causal ancestry that competent practitioners already share through the confessional and liturgical grammar, (ii) the predicate yields a determinate exclusion of creaturely modes, and (iii) the predicate supports a determinate positive inference about what God wills, does, or is not, so that two practitioners who accept the same grammar will sort the same predications into the same categories. Against this standard the paper would examine the Ruth and Nadia case as a test of the remotion-plus-eminence account, not as a refutation of the Fregean premise. Essay B is correctly excluded by the modus significandi rule, and the paper would grant that. The test is whether the account can also sort predications that share causal ancestry but differ in creaturely paradigm, such as 'God is just' and 'God is fair', or 'God is wise' and 'God is shrewd'. The paper would argue that remotion and eminence settle the direction of the predicate but leave the choice among causally grounded candidates to a judgement that the doctrine does not specify. That gap, not an absence of content, is where the sufficiency claim is weakest. The paper would engage the Burrell, McInerny, Alston and Ross literature on this exact gap, separating the question of whether analogy is equivocal from the question of whether it is agnostic, and it would show where the sufficiency claim needs either a further criterion of proportional ratio or a restriction in its scope. The contribution is a diagnosis of where the determinacy claim stops, measured by a standard internal to the doctrine.

**How the paper answers the main objection:** The referee is right that the original paper attacked a claim the argument does not make, and that the demand for criteria usable without a prior grasp of the divine nature begs the question against Thomism. The revision retargets the paper to c042 and c005, the claims about sufficient determinacy, and drops the charge that c009 fails. It replies to the 'semantic openness' concession by making the thesis a claim about the degree of determinacy, not about its presence. The standard is set by the doctrine's own commitments: determinacy of the perfection signified, determinacy of creaturely-mode exclusion, and determinacy of positive inference shared by a community of practice. The Ruth case is retained only as a test that remotion and eminence pass for exclusion and may fail for choice among causally grounded predicates. The paper answers the referee's concern about operational determinacy by specifying it, not by assuming it.
