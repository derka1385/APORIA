## Challenged premise
**W4405407480.arg1.r3**: The truthmaker of 'God creates world W' must include the identity of W as the object fixed by God's volition, so that the content of the volition is partly determined by a contingent worldly fact rather than by God's intrinsic nature alone.

## Objection
*blind_thought_experimenter · anthropic:sonnet*

Consider Ada, a potter who has resolved to make one bowl and no other, before any clay exists. Her resolve is fixed by her settled character and plan; the content 'make bowl B' is picked out by her intention alone, and B's specific identity is not yet anything. When clay is delivered, B comes to exist, and the sentence 'Ada made B' becomes true. Her intention did not change; what changed is that a contingent object now satisfies a description her intrinsic intention already determined. The truthmaker of 'Ada made B' includes B, yet the content of her volition, 'make the bowl that suits this plan,' was fixed without reference to any worldly fact. Analogously, God's volition can have a content specified intrinsically, such as 'bring about whatever world satisfies this plan,' with the identity of W entering only in the truth of 'God creates W,' not in the content of the volition. The revised premise conflates the truthmaker of an attribution with the determinant of the volition's content, and the case shows these come apart.

## Open questions
- Does the inclusion of a contingent worldly particular in the truthmaker of an attribution suffice to make the attributed property partially extrinsic in a sense that threatens divine simplicity, or does the Ada analogy show that truthmaker inclusion can be benign?
- Can an intrinsically specified divine plan contingently select a unique world without either collapsing into necessary creation or tacitly making the volition's content world-indexed?
- If the content of God's volition is fixed independently of any worldly fact, what account of truthmaking for accidental divine attributions preserves both the contingency of creation and the doctrine that God's minimal truthmakers include only God?

## Paper direction
A paper here would argue that the modal collapse argument against divine simplicity fails because it conflates the truthmaker of an accidental attribution with the intrinsic content of the volition it attributes. The case would develop the Ada analogy to show that an agent can have an intrinsically fixed volition whose content—such as 'make the bowl that satisfies this plan' or 'bring about whatever world satisfies this plan'—does not include any worldly particular, even though the truth of the resulting attribution depends on which contingent object later satisfies that description. The paper would anticipate Defender B's reply that the property of creating world W remains extrinsic simply because W figures in the truthmaker, and that a general intrinsic plan either makes creation necessary or reintroduces worldly variation; it would need to show either that truthmaker inclusion by itself does not yield the metaphysical extrinsicness required to threaten divine simplicity, or that an intrinsic plan can non-arbitrarily select a unique possible satisfier without collapsing modality or secretly indexing its content to the world.

The paper retargets its critique to c009, which is a pro-simplicity response to modal collapse, and not to the modal collapse argument itself. It argues that the Ada analogy fails as a model of contingency because Ada's selection depends on externally delivered clay, which God lacks. The truthmaker/content distinction is therefore at most a necessary part of the reply, not a sufficient one. The paper's narrower claim is that truthmaker inclusion of W does not by itself establish the metaphysical extrinsicness that threatens simplicity, so an extrinsic-truthmaker reply preserves the truthmaker requirement only if the relational character of 'creates W' is shown not to be intrinsic in the sense that matters. It engages the extrinsic-model literature (Grant, Tomaszewski, Mullins, Schmid) on exactly this question. It does not claim to show how an intrinsic plan selects a unique world without collapse; it names that as the residual burden and states the conditions under which the burden would be met.

**How the paper answers the main objection:** The paper concedes the referee's central point: the Ada case restates the dilemma and cannot supply contingency from within the will. It replies in four steps. First, it retargets the critique to c009, so the thesis no longer claims that the modal collapse argument fails. Second, it separates two senses of 'extrinsic': truthmaker inclusion (a relation to W) and dependence of the volition's content on W. It argues that the collapse threat requires the second sense, and that r3 runs the two together. Third, it shows that if content is intrinsic and necessary, contingency of creation lies in the relational truth of 'creates W', so the inference from extrinsic truthmaker to extrinsic property must be tested directly. Fourth, it leaves unique selection by an intrinsic plan as an open burden and does not claim to have discharged it.
