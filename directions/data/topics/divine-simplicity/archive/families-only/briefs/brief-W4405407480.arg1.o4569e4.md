## Challenged premise
**W4405407480.c013**: Any correct attribution to God of an accidental property has as its truthmaker God and something contingent besides God, so the property is at least partially extrinsic.

## Objection
*tradition_lens · gemma:google/gemma-4-26B-A4B-it*

Consider a person, Leo, who decides to drink water. The truth of 'Leo decides to drink water' is made true by Leo's internal volition. This volition is a property of Leo. The truthmaker is not Leo plus an external, contingent fact; the truthmaker is simply Leo, as a person possessing that specific mental state. Similarly, God's volition is a real, distinct attribute of His personhood. The contingency resides within the property God possesses, not in a fact that exists 'besides' Him. Therefore, an accidental property is not an extrinsic addition but an intrinsic part of God's person.

## Open questions
- Can a contingent, world-directed content be an intrinsic feature of a volitional property in a simple being without introducing an extrinsic truthmaker?
- Does the necessity of the divine essence entail the necessity of every intrinsic feature of that essence, or can an act identical to God possess contingent determinations without rendering God contingent?
- How should the minimal truthmaker for 'God freely chooses to create the actual world' be construed if the contingency of the chosen world is located within God's will rather than in the world itself?

## Paper direction
A paper here would argue that the modal-collapse objection to divine simplicity can be blocked by developing an intrinsic theory of divine volition on which the contingent, world-directed content of God's will is a feature of the volitional property itself rather than an extrinsic addition, so that the minimal truthmaker for 'God freely chooses to create the actual world' remains God alone. The case would proceed by defending the Leo analogy against the charge that world-directedness entails extrinsicality, showing that a simple act-identical-to-God can have contingent content without making God contingent, and distinguishing the modality of the divine essence from the modality of its intrinsic contingent determinations. The strongest reply to anticipate is that if the content is intrinsic and God is necessary, then the content is necessary and modal collapse follows; the paper would need to show that this assumes an illicit inference from the necessity of the subject to the necessity of every intrinsic feature, or that the contingency of the content is grounded in the intrinsic property without requiring an external truthmaker, thereby preserving both divine simplicity and the contingency of creation.

**Revised question:** Given divine simplicity, can a divine truth about God's contingent free choice be made true by God alone without being necessitated by its truthmaker, so that the contingency of creation is located in the truth rather than in any intrinsic or extrinsic determination of God?

A paper here would argue that the modal-collapse objection to divine simplicity is blocked only by rejecting truthmaker necessitation for divine contingent truths, not by an intrinsic theory of volition. The Leo analogy is abandoned: 'Leo decides' is made true by Leo-in-a-volitional-state, a state distinct from Leo that could have been absent while Leo existed, so it is a composite truthmaker and cannot model a simple being. The paper also rejects the reply that inferring the necessity of an intrinsic feature from the necessity of its subject is illicit, since for a simple being the feature is the subject. The paper then argues that the best remaining option keeps the minimal-truthmaker thesis (c012) for God. 'God freely chooses to create the actual world' has God alone as its minimal truthmaker, but that truthmaker does not necessitate the proposition, because its truth also depends on the contingent relation of God's free choice to a contingent relatum. The contingency is thus located in the truth-conditions and the modal profile of the relation, not in any intrinsic determination of God. The paper defends this non-necessitation claim against the charge that bare-God truthmaking without necessitation is ad hoc, showing that it follows from a principled account of how a necessary being can ground contingent truths about its relations. It engages the existing extrinsic-model and non-necessitarian literature, namely the work associated with Brower, Grant, Tomaszewski, Pruss, Mullins and Schmid, and it positions the non-necessitarian option against those accounts. It does not claim to provide a new intrinsic account of contingent divine volition, and it does not claim to show that the simplicity thesis is weaker than its defenders take it to be.

**How the paper answers the main objection:** The referee argues that the Leo analogy equivocates on the truthmaker, that God-alone truthmaking plus necessitation yields collapse, and that the illicit-inference rejoinder fails for a simple being. The paper accepts the first and third points. It drops the Leo analogy and the intrinsic-volition thesis, so the paper no longer requires an intrinsic contingent determination distinct from God. It answers the dilemma by denying its first horn: truthmaker necessitation fails for divine contingent truths. God alone is the minimal truthmaker for 'God chooses W', which preserves c012, but God's existence does not necessitate the truth, because that truth depends on the contingent relatum W. The paper defends this against the ad hoc charge by showing that non-necessitation follows from the relational structure of free choice. Because the contingent factor is not an extrinsic truthmaker either, the second horn is avoided without conceding c013 in its intrinsic form.

If W's existence partly settles whether the proposition is true, then W is part of what makes it true, and that is exactly c013's extrinsic model under a new label. If W is not part of what makes it true, then nothing in reality accounts for the difference between worlds where the proposition holds and worlds where it fails, and that is brute non-necessitation. Brute non-necessitation abandons the explanatory point of truthmaking (Lewis/Armstrong: truth supervenes on being) and is the familiar 'unexplained contingency' option already worked out by Pruss and by Schmid. So the paper either concedes the challenged premise or restates existing non-necessitarian proposals.
- What it still needs: It must give a principled account of truthmaking without necessitation. That account has to explain why dependence on W is not partial truthmaking by W, and why truth-supervenes-on-being is not violated, rather than simply asserting that the contingency sits 'in the truth-conditions'. It must also show how this differs from the extrinsic models of Grant and Brower and from the non-necessitarian proposals of Pruss, Tomaszewski and Schmid.
