## Challenged premise
**W3115675310.c004**: Consequently, there would be only one divine attribute if all attributes are identical.

## Objection
*blind_thought_experimenter · kimi:moonshotai/Kimi-K2.6*

In the video game Apex, the final boss's hit points, attack power, and speed are all coded as the exact same integer, 999, and are not distinct variables—each attribute just is that core value. On the stat screen, however, players see three separate bars labeled HP, ATK, and SPD. The designers do not call this one attribute; they treat it as three distinct attributes that share one identical ground. The players plan around three different abilities. Therefore, even when attributes are strictly identical in their underlying reality, it does not follow that there is only one attribute.

## Strongest responses and why they failed
- **Defender A**: The Apex case is a fair counterexample to a naive inference from identity of ground to identity of attribute-count, but it does not establish that c004 is false as stated because the analogy does not preserve the feature that matters: in the game, HP, ATK, and SPD are distinct at the level of presentation and function, showing that many predicates can share one ground without collapsing into one predicate. The objection therefore shows that the count of attributes depends on individuation, and c004 is vulnerable because it lacks an individuation premise. — *Defender A conceded that c004 as stated does not survive the objection, since the inference from identity to a single attribute requires an individuation premise that the argument does not supply, though Defender A suggested the objection may not reach the broader worries in c005 and c006 if attributes are distinct only by sense.*
- **Defender B**: The Apex boss has three genuinely distinct attributes—hit points, attack power, and speed—each with a different account, meaning, and scope, so they are not strictly identical; the analogy thus illustrates distinct attributes grounded in one value rather than showing that strict identity permits plurality. — *The objector replied that if difference in meaning is sufficient to underwrite plurality in the game, the divine simplicity theorist may invoke the same distinction for divine attributes, and if it is insufficient, the analogy survives; Defender B conceded that this exposes a genuine gap and that c004 smuggles in an undefended assumption that ontological identity precludes conceptual distinction.*

## Open questions
- If divine attributes are individuated only by sense or predicative role, does the objection from multiplicity in c005 and the abstractness worry in c006 retain any independent force?
- Can the critic independently motivate the revised bridge premise that ontological oneness precludes any difference in meaning, account, or scope without begging the question against the Ash'arite distinction between ontological identity and conceptual plurality?
- Does the Apex analogy accurately model the metaphysical relation between the divine essence and attributes, or do disanalogies between a coded integer and a metaphysically simple substance weaken the objection's extension to the divine case?
- Is sense-based individuation metaphysically sufficient for genuine theological plurality, or does it reduce divine attributes to mere modes of presentation without distinct causal or explanatory roles?

## Paper direction
A paper here would argue that the inference from divine simplicity to the oneness of divine attributes is invalid unless supplemented by a controversial premise that ontological identity precludes any difference in meaning, account, or scope. The case would develop the Apex analogy to demonstrate that strict identity of an underlying ground is compatible with genuine plurality of attributes when they are individuated by sense or functional role, and it would contend that the Ash'arite strategy of distinguishing divine attributes by their distinct accounts is sufficient to block the collapse into a single attribute. The strongest reply to anticipate is that the game analogy trades on merely accidental sameness of numerical value rather than the metaphysical sameness of essence and attribute, or that conceptual distinction without ontological difference cannot yield theologically robust plurality. To succeed, the paper would need to show that sense-based individuation is metaphysically coherent in the divine case and that the critic's demand for ontological differentiation among attributes begs the question against the simplicity theorist's framework.
