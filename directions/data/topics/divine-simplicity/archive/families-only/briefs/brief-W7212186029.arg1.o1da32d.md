## Challenged premise

## Objection
*blind_thought_experimenter · anthropic:sonnet*

Let the domain be {a, b}, where a is part of b, b is part of a, and a ≠ b. Define proper part as parthood that is not mutual, so neither a nor b has a proper part. Take extensionality in the restricted form that applies only to things with proper parts: anything with proper parts is fixed by them. This restricted principle is vacuous for a and b, since neither has proper parts. The theory is therefore extensional in the sense the restriction supplies, yet it permits distinct mutual parts. Divergence between the identity and symmetry formulations occurs here: a and b lack distinct non-mutual parts, so the identity formulation's 'no distinct parts' condition is met, while the symmetry formulation's 'no non-symmetric parts' condition is also met, but a and b are distinct, so identifying God with all his parts fails. The restatement r1 assigns divergence to 'non-extensional' theories, but this extensional-by-restriction theory diverges too.

A paper here would argue that the divergence between the identity and symmetry formulations of divine simplicity requires distinct mutual parts, and that this is not the same as failure of extensionality for proper parts. The paper would draw two notions of proper part apart. The classical notion counts x as a proper part of y when x is part of y and x is distinct from y. The symmetry formulation uses the non-symmetric notion, on which proper parthood excludes mutual parthood. Proper-parts extensionality, stated with the classical notion (anything with classical proper parts is fixed by them), is non-vacuous for a two-element model with a and b distinct and mutually part of each other. Here a has classical proper part b and b has classical proper part a. Their classical proper-part sets, {b} and {a}, differ, so the principle is satisfied and is not idle. The paper would then state the model in full. Its parthood relation is reflexive, transitive and mutual, with a and b distinct. It satisfies proper-parts extensionality on the classical notion and violates antisymmetry and full extensionality (same parts, distinct things). In this model the identity formulation fails: a has the distinct part b. The symmetry formulation holds: a has no non-symmetric parts, because its only parts are itself and the mutual part b. The paper would argue that, given reflexivity and transitivity, distinct mutual parts and failure of full extensionality are equivalent, so the correct restatement of r1 is that divergence requires a mereology permitting distinct mutual parts, that is, failure of antisymmetry. The label 'non-extensional' should be reserved for failure of full extensionality, and the paper would concede that the model is non-extensional in that sense while satisfying the classical proper-parts principle. The paper would also defend pairing the classical notion with extensionality and the non-symmetric notion with the doctrine. Extensionality concerns individuation by parts, so it should use the notion that tracks distinct parts; on the non-symmetric notion the principle is vacuous for the model, as the referee noted. The paper would then state the cost of the symmetry formulation, namely that its distinctness from the identity formulation is bought by admitting distinct mutual parts, and assess whether that cost is acceptable for the viability claim.
