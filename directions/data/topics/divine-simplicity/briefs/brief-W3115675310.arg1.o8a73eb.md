## Challenged premise
**W3115675310.arg1.r1**: Multiplicity of divine attributes must be ontologically real (differences in being, not only in sense or description), since only real multiplicity makes the attributes' non-identity a datum that simplicity must deny.

## Objection
*reasoner:explorer · glm:zai-org/GLM-5.2*

Consider a community of theologians who independently derive two characterizations of God: one through reflection on divine power (omnipotence), another through reflection on divine knowledge (omniscience). These are genuinely distinct conceptual achievements, reached through different epistemic routes, with different explanatory roles. The theologians can distinguish the concepts, apply them separately, and even wonder whether a single being could satisfy both. Now suppose divine simplicity is true and the attributes are ontologically identical. The conceptual multiplicity remains a real datum: the two concepts are not interchangeable, and the fact that both apply to the same being is non-trivial and demands explanation. Yet this multiplicity is not ontological—there is no difference in being between the attributes. The distinctness lies in sense and cognitive role, not in reality. The premise claims only ontological multiplicity yields a datum simplicity must address, but here conceptual multiplicity itself constitutes a datum requiring account, even with no ontological difference.

## Open questions
- Can a semantics of divine predication be developed that explains how one simple reality satisfies the heterogeneous conditions associated with distinct divine attributes without tacitly reintroducing ontological complexity?
- Is the Fregean sense–reference distinction applicable to divine attributes when those attributes carry irreducibly distinct explanatory commitments rather than functioning as mere alternative modes of presentation?
- Does admitting conceptual multiplicity with distinct satisfaction conditions as a datum that simplicity must accommodate undermine the ontological purity of divine simplicity, or can the two be cleanly separated?

## Paper direction
A paper here would argue that the reductio against divine simplicity from the multiplicity of divine attributes requires a premise establishing that the distinct satisfaction conditions and explanatory roles of attributes such as omnipotence and omniscience cannot be discharged by the Fregean sense–reference distinction alone. The case would proceed by showing that treating conceptual multiplicity as a mere difference in sense presupposes rather than proves that one simple reality can satisfy heterogeneous conditions, and that the objector’s scenario of theologians deriving attributes through independent epistemic routes generates a genuine datum—distinct explanatory commitments—that simplicity must accommodate. The strongest reply to anticipate is the Fregean maneuver itself, namely that conceptual difference never entails ontological difference and that sense–reference successfully explains co-reference without ontological cost. To succeed, the paper would need to show either that the sense–reference model cannot be extended to predicates with distinct satisfaction conditions without tacitly reintroducing complexity, or that the defender of simplicity must offer a positive semantic theory of divine predication that independently justifies why heterogeneous explanatory roles do not entail distinct properties in the divine essence.

A paper here would argue that the reductio of divine simplicity from the multiplicity of divine attributes should be restated as a demand on truthmakers or grounds for distinct predications, not as a demand that simplicity deny conceptual or explanatory data. The premise that ontological multiplicity must be real is retained only in the form the referee accepts: simplicity must deny ontological difference among the attributes. What the objector's scenario shows is that distinct true predications require something in the divine reality to make them true. The paper would test three positive accounts against that demand: Brower's truthmaker account, the Thomistic doctrine of analogical predication, and the Ash'arite sifat formula. For each account it would ask whether the truthmaker for 'God is omnipotent' and the truthmaker for 'God is omniscient' can be distinct items in the divine reality without reintroducing ontological composition, and whether the account can state that difference without appeal to a distinct property. The Fregean sense-reference distinction is no longer the target. It figures only as a secondary consideration, showing why conceptual multiplicity alone does not settle the truthmaker question. The paper would conclude by identifying which account, if any, meets the grounding demand without tacit complexity, and what that verdict implies for the reductio.
