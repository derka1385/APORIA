## Challenged premise

## Objection
*blind_thought_experimenter · kimi:moonshotai/Kimi-K2.6*

The world Sky contains conscious birds who feel pleasure soaring on wind currents that follow an exceptionally elegant physical law. The birds are not capable of mathematical appreciation and do not value abstract structure; moreover, no God or external Being exists in Sky to appreciate the law’s elegance. Because the mathematical elegance of the airflow directly produces smoother, more joyful flight, it increases total pleasure minus suffering among Sky’s conscious inhabitants. The elegance therefore augments the world’s total goodness even though no suitable Being appreciates it.

## Strongest responses and why they failed
- **Defender A**: In Sky the elegance does its work by producing smoother flight, so its contribution is exhausted by the pleasure it causes; that is instrumental value routed through the conscious experiences already counted in the experiential total, not a separate addend, and the objection therefore does not show a non-relational intrinsic contribution that would break the link to God. — *The defender concedes that the stated premises do not, on their own, license the conclusion that a God exists, and must supply a revised premise restricting elegance's added value to what appreciation or its effects supply, thereby acknowledging that the original argument fails without alteration.*
- **Defender B**: Elegance in Sky raises total goodness instrumentally by increasing the birds' pleasure, not by contributing non-instrumental aesthetic value; under an infinite-value interpretation, finite increments of bird pleasure could never substitute for the axiological contribution of divine appreciation, so Sky shows only instrumental value and not the aesthetic value the argument needs God to supply. — *The defender is forced to retreat from a claim about total goodness to a narrower claim about non-instrumental aesthetic value that was not originally distinguished in the premises, effectively conceding that the objection undermines the premise as stated and requires substantial revision.*

## Open questions
- Can the instrumental–non-instrumental distinction be applied to mathematical elegance without rendering the revised premise ad hoc?
- Does defining non-instrumental aesthetic value as requiring a suitable Being covertly presuppose the existence of God rather than establishing it?
- If divine appreciation carries infinite axiological weight, can finite conscious experiences contribute non-negligibly to the goodness of the best possible world?

## Paper direction
A paper here would argue that the defenders' retreat to an instrumental–non-instrumental distinction fails to salvage the inference from the best-possible-world hypothesis to the existence of God. The case would develop the Sky counterexample to show that mathematical elegance can augment a world's goodness through its effects on conscious experience without any suitable appreciator, and would then argue that the revised premise restricting non-instrumental aesthetic value to appreciated elegance introduces an ad hoc distinction that was not operative in the original argument. The strongest reply to anticipate is that the argument always concerned non-instrumental aesthetic value and that Sky therefore misses its target. To succeed, the paper would need to show either that the distinction cannot be coherently applied to mathematical elegance, or that even non-instrumental value does not entail a transcendent valuer, or that the revised framework renders the argument question-begging by making the existence of a suitable Being a definitional precondition for the kind of value that makes the actual world best.

A paper here would argue that the inference from the best-possible-world hypothesis to an omniscient, omnipotent, omnibenevolent God fails on either reading of the intrinsic value that premise c010 assigns to mathematical elegance. The paper drops the Sky counterexample as its central case. Its work starts from the gap between c008, which defines total goodness as the pleasure-minus-suffering total of conscious experiences, and c010, which adds a further intrinsic contribution from elegance. Any contribution that is not experiential falls outside c008's total, so the paper must say what it is and where it enters the goodness of the world. The paper then develops a dilemma. If the intrinsic contribution depends on an appreciating valuer, a divine appreciator is a precondition of the value that makes the actual world best, so the conclusion that God exists is built into the premise that the world is best, and the inference is question-begging. If the contribution does not depend on an appreciator, as the Lattice clause in the challenged premise suggests, nothing in the argument supplies a creator or valuer, and arg1.mp, which is unstated, does not bridge the step from elegance-value to a God who creates and values the universe. The paper engages the literature on objective aesthetic value and on value-to-whom accounts to decide which horn is stronger. Its claim is limited: it does not show that theism is false, only that this argument does not establish it.

**How the paper answers the main objection:** The paper accepts the referee's point that Sky shows only causal, experiential contributions, which c008 already counts, and that c010 speaks of intrinsic value, so the instrumental/intrinsic distinction is in the original argument and not an ad hoc retreat. The paper therefore does not rest on Sky. It targets the claim the referee identifies: that elegance makes a non-experiential intrinsic contribution. Its reply has two steps. First, it shows that c008 and c010 together leave that contribution outside the experiential total, so the paper must state what the contribution is and where it is counted. Second, it argues that the answer forces a choice. If the contribution needs an appreciator, the divine valuer is a definitional precondition and the inference is question-begging. If it does not, the argument yields no God. The paper does not depend on the coherence of the instrumental/intrinsic distinction for elegance, because the dilemma holds for whichever kind of value c010 names.
