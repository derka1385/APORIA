## Challenged premise
**W2124966002.arg1.r1**: The Jamesian model supplies what faith needs only where the passionally motivated tendency to believe is stabilised into believingly accepting the unestablished claim, and c031 should be restricted accordingly.

## Objection
*blind_thought_experimenter · anthropic:sonnet*

Consider Marta, a hospice chaplain who, after her sister's death, is passionately drawn to believe that God holds the dead in mercy. She acts on this tendency: she prays, sings the hymns, and tells the grieving family she is sure. Yet when a colleague asks whether she is certain the afterlife exists, she says plainly that she is not; she holds the claim as a live hope she would abandon if evidence turned against it. Her tendency to believe has not stabilised into believingly accepting the claim as settled, since she still regards it as unestablished and revisable. Nonetheless, her faith is robust, lived, and assurance-laden in the practical sense her community means by it. The restriction in arg1.r1 says the Jamesian model supplies what faith needs only where stabilisation occurs, so Marta's case is excluded from the model's scope. But her case shows that the faith-relevant work is done by the passionate tendency together with committed action, not by stabilised doxastic acceptance. The stabilisation condition therefore restricts the model to cases where it is not needed.

## Open questions
- Can practical assurance generated by committed passional action be phenomenologically distinguished from the assurance that supposedly requires settled belief?
- Does dropping the stabilisation condition from the Jamesian model collapse the distinction between doxastic venture and sub-doxastic hope or assumption?
- If faith’s core is an unstabilised tendency expressed in action, does the Jamesian model retain any advantage over the pragmatic or hope-based accounts it was meant to supplant?

## Paper direction
A paper here would argue that the Jamesian doxastic-venture model loses its claimed phenomenological superiority once the stabilisation condition is removed, because the revised, unstabilised account cannot distinguish faith from the attenuated sub-doxastic views it was meant to supplant. The case would centre on cases like Marta’s, where practical assurance is present without doxastic settlement, to show that assurance-in-action is insufficient to mark a distinctly doxastic venture and that c030’s requirement of belief and certitude is undermined rather than satisfied by dropping stabilisation. The strongest reply to anticipate is the revised premise that committed passional tendency alone constitutes faith’s assured core; the paper would need to show that this either tacitly reintroduces a form of acceptance equivalent to stabilised belief, or else concedes that the model collapses into a sub-doxastic hope account and therefore cannot claim phenomenological superiority over its rivals.

## Revised after the quality check
*The revision drops the stabilisation-based collapse thesis and the claim of phenomenological superiority, and reframes the paper around an inferential-role criterion that tests whether Marta-type assurance is believing acceptance or hope.* The thesis was narrowed.

**Revised question:** On the Jamesian doxastic-venture model, does the assurance-laden core of Christian faith require believing acceptance in the practical-reasoning sense, and can an independent test distinguish such acceptance from hope in cases like Marta's?

A paper here would argue that the stabilisation restriction in arg1.r1 misreads the doxastic-venture model, because on that model believing acceptance of an unestablished claim is taking p to be true in practical reasoning while recognising that the evidence does not settle p. The paper would therefore drop the claim that Marta's case falls outside the model's scope and would instead test whether her case falls inside it. It would give an independent criterion for the difference between believing acceptance and hope: believing acceptance treats p as a premise in practical and assertoric reasoning, so that the agent answers questions about p from p and reasons from it to conclusions such as what to say and do; hope takes the desired outcome as the object of motivation, with action conditioned on p without p functioning as a premise. Credence-level reports, such as 'I am not certain', are relevant evidence but do not settle which state is present, since the inferential role is what does the work. Applied to Marta, the criterion yields a split: her assertion to the family that she is sure, and her reasoning from the afterlife claim in her ministry, are believing-acceptance evidence, while her disavowal of certainty and her reluctance to treat p as settled are hope evidence. The paper would argue that the Marta-type case is a real test only if these two strands can be separated in an actual agent, and that where they cannot, the model is committed to classifying the agent as believing beyond evidence. The paper's claim about phenomenological superiority in c047 is withdrawn in favour of the narrower claim that assurance-laden faith, read through inferential role, is a doxastic venture rather than a sub-doxastic hope.

**How the paper answers the main objection:** The referee is right that the revised premise is a construction no defender holds, and that on the model's own notion 'regards it as unestablished' is compatible with believing acceptance. The paper therefore concedes the coherence point and drops the stabilisation restriction. It answers the robustness point by supplying an independent criterion, inferential role in practical and assertoric reasoning, which separates believing acceptance from hope without relying on the belief/acceptance labels. It answers the specificity point by stating how the test applies to Marta. It concedes the significance point: the claim that a non-belief account is not doxastic is near-analytic, so the paper's contribution lies in the criterion and in the case analysis, not in the thesis that non-doxastic faith is non-doxastic.

*A tidy acceptance-versus-hope criterion that leaves untouched the real problem, distinguishing doxastic venture from sub-doxastic acceptance, so the first competent reader would note that it reproduces Cohen's acceptance and send it back.*

- Strongest remaining objection: The inferential-role criterion separates the wrong pair of states. Treating p as a premise in practical and assertoric reasoning while recognising the evidence does not settle p is the standard definition of non-doxastic acceptance (Cohen, Bratman), which is exactly the 'overassumption' that c046 and c030 contrast with overbelief. So the criterion can at best show that Marta accepts rather than merely hopes. It cannot show that her acceptance is doxastic. That is precisely the unanswered dilemma from the trial: what makes the tendency belief rather than sub-doxastic assumption. The rule that inseparable cases count as 'believing beyond evidence' simply stipulates the answer.
- What it still needs: The paper must supply a mark of belief, not merely of acceptance, that is independent of inferential role. Candidates include involuntariness, a truth-directed correctness norm, or the persistence of the state outside deliberative contexts. It must then show that Marta-type agents can meet it while sincerely disavowing certainty, and it must say whether her 'I am sure' to the family is sincere assertion or pastoral performance.
