## Objection
*blind_thought_experimenter · kimi:moonshotai/Kimi-K2.6*

Consider an eternal being, Alpha, for whom all times are present. At the eternal now, Jones's choice of cake over pie is fully present. Alpha's mind, however, hosts a timeless internal mirror that automatically duplicates every temporal event. Alpha's conscious state tracks this duplicate, not the act itself. If the duplicate were generated by a glitch while the act failed to occur, Alpha would still register "Jones chooses cake." Thus Alpha's state is merely a correlated disposition toward the duplicate. The actual choice is eternally present, but presence alone does not make Alpha's cognition answerable to the real act; it remains answerable to the internal copy. Therefore, eternal presence does not suffice to establish that knowledge of a temporal act is knowledge of the act itself rather than a correlated disposition.

**Revised question:** Under what determinate, non-circular condition is God's eternal presence to a temporal act a presence-to relation that makes divine knowledge answerable to the act itself rather than to a surrogate?

A paper here would argue that the mirror case refutes the claim that eternal co-presence alone makes divine knowledge answerable to a temporal act, and the paper concedes this narrower point. Premise r2 is narrowed accordingly: co-presence of act and knower is necessary but not sufficient for knowledge of the act itself. What is sufficient is presence-to, understood as unmediated acquaintance in which the content of God's cognitive state is fixed by the act and by nothing intervening. The paper gives this a determinate criterion, a counterfactual dependence condition: had the act not occurred, the cognitive state would not register that act. The mirror case fails this condition because its registration persists when the act is absent through a glitch, so it is a case of mediation rather than presence-to. The paper then argues that the Grounding Principle for creaturely agents is satisfied under this criterion, since the truth of the free choice and God's knowledge of it both depend on the actual choice, and that an internal necessity of the form If X then X does not undermine that dependence. It engages Hasker's challenge by testing it against the presence-to criterion rather than against bare co-presence. It states how its criterion differs from the ET-simultaneity and Anselmian positions: it makes explicit a test for telling mediated from unmediated cases, and applies that test to the mirror case instead of assuming unmediated acquaintance as a premise. The reconciliation thesis holds only if this condition is met, so the paper's contribution is the condition and its application.

*The question is the right one and the plan is clear, but the proposed criterion is a sensitivity condition that reliable mirrors pass and divine infallibility trivializes, so as it stands the first competent referee would reject it.*

- Strongest remaining objection: The proposed counterfactual criterion cannot tell mediated from unmediated cognition, so it fails at the one job it is given. First, it is too weak. A perfectly reliable mirror with no glitch possibility passes it, because had the act not occurred the duplicate would not have occurred and nothing would be registered. That makes the mirror case's failure an artifact of stipulated unreliability, not of mediation. This is the familiar lesson from sensitivity accounts: reliable causal intermediaries satisfy counterfactual dependence. Second, applied to God, the counterfactual is settled by divine infallibility. On any account, including a mediated or dispositional one, God's state would not register an act that did not occur. So either the criterion is trivially met by everything, or it quietly relies on the knowledge relation it was meant to ground. That is the circularity the paper claims to have escaped.
- What it still needs: Replace or supplement the counterfactual test with a condition that rules out reliable intermediaries, such as a constitution or no-intervening-relatum condition, and show that this condition is not satisfied for free by God's essential omniscience. It must also handle the backtracking-counterfactual worries that drive Hasker's challenge, rather than presupposing their resolution.
