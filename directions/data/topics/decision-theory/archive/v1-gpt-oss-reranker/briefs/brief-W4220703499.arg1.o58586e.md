## Objection
*tradition_lens · gemma:google/gemma-4-26B-A4B-it*

Imagine a man named Elias who chooses to drink water at noon. According to the Grounding Principle, the truth of this choice and the knowledge of it are grounded in Elias's act. If God's knowledge of Elias's choice depends on that contingent act, then God's knowledge is not identical to His necessary essence. Since God is absolutely simple, His knowledge is His being. If His knowledge is grounded in a creature's choice, then God's very existence becomes contingent upon the world, violating divine simplicity and aseity.
