## Objection
*naive_questioner+sharpener · gemma→gemma:google/gemma-4-26B-A4B-it→google/gemma-4-26B-A4B-it*

Elias experiences faith as a state of absolute, unshakable certitude. The model defines faith as 'believingly accepting an unestablished claim.' To accept a claim as unestablished requires a psychological awareness of its lack of proof. This awareness of epistemic contingency is fundamentally incompatible with the phenomenological state of absolute certitude. If the mechanism of faith is the acceptance of the unestablished, it cannot produce the specific sense of total assurance required. W2124966002.c031 fails because accepting an unestablished claim is psychologically incompatible with the state of certitude it is intended to supply.

## Strongest responses and why they failed
- **Defender A**: The venture consists in the act of committing before proof, and the subsequent feeling of assurance is a later phenomenological state, not a simultaneous one. — *This temporal separation leaves the original claim that venture and assurance co‑occur unsubstantiated, so the gap identified by the objector remains.*
- **Defender B**: Faith is a passionally based tendency that objectively outstrips the evidence while generating occurrent certitude, without the believer consciously accepting the claim as unestablished. — *By relegating the unestablishedness to an objective fact, the reply evades the required first‑person awareness of risk, failing to resolve the phenomenological incompatibility.*

## Open questions
