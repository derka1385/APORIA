## Objection

World A contains one hundred humans wired to a flawless pleasure machine. Every moment is engineered delight; no suffering exists; their pleasure-minus-suffering score is one million. World B contains one hundred humans living real lives: they love, raise children, discover truths, and create art, but they also feel grief, cold, and failure, yielding a score of five hundred thousand. Everyone who grasps both worlds prefers B. The inhabitants of B would refuse to enter A even if promised tripled pleasure, because they value authentic connection and real accomplishment over raw feeling. Since World B is genuinely better despite a lower pleasure-minus-suffering total, total goodness cannot be identical to that sum.

The paper would first set out a dilemma. If value is hedonic, the deficit inference holds, but the Worlds A and B case must be met on its merits. The paper would take the second horn. It would then treat the bridging premise from best world to God's existence as a separate section, since that premise is the argument's weakest point. The Everett branch-aggregation question would be stated as a dependency that the conclusion relies on and that this paper does not settle.
