## Challenged premise
**W4220703499.arg1.r1**: God's knowing is ungrounded in creaturely acts while its objects are grounded in them.

## Objection
*blind_thought_experimenter · anthropic:sonnet*

Consider Maren, a creature who at noon on Tuesday freely decides to keep a promise rather than break it. God, eternally present to all time, knows at dawn that Maren will keep it. Suppose God's knowing at dawn is grounded in nothing creaturely: it is wholly self-originating. Now ask what it is for that knowing to be the knowing that Maren keeps the promise. Its object is fixed by a fact that holds only because Maren's noon decision occurs. If the object is grounded in her act while the knowing is not, then the knowing is correct without being answerable to anything in the world, and it would be correct even had Maren broken the promise, provided the object had been different. So the knowing's content varies with her act while the knowing does not, which means it is a knowing of nothing in particular: a dispositional state that happens to be co-extensive with Maren's act. The premise stipulates an asymmetry that cannot be stated as knowledge at all.

A paper here would argue that the dispositional charge against eternal divine knowledge equivocates on 'grounded', and that once the two senses are separated, eternal knowledge of a noon free act is factive knowledge that satisfies a non-causal answerability condition, without any novel metaphysics of eternity. The paper would proceed in four steps. First, it fixes the operative sense of 'grounded' in the Anselmian aseity framework: dependence that is causal or passive and bears on divine aseity. Second, it states the objection in atemporal terms. The challenged premise (W4220703499.arg1.r1) holds that God's knowing is ungrounded in creaturely acts in this sense while its objects are grounded in them. The paper drops the 'at dawn' formulation, since on the eternalist view God knows the noon choice by being atemporally present to it. Third, it concedes the content-dependence the objector relies on. The Grounding Principle (W4220703499.c007) already says that the truth and knowledge of a free choice depend on the actual choice, so the objection's asymmetry is coherent only as a contrast between content-dependence and causal or passive dependence. The claim that content varies while the knowing does not is withdrawn, because it is incoherent if mental states are individuated by content. Fourth, the paper gives a criterion that does not presuppose the answer. A state S is knowledge of p, rather than a mere co-extensive disposition, when (a) S's correctness conditions are fixed by whether p obtains, and (b) S would be assessed as incorrect were p otherwise, so that the difference in content marks a different state. Counterfactual covariation of this kind is the sensitivity profile of ordinary factive knowledge. The paper argues that eternal presence meets (a) and (b) with respect to Maren's noon act, and that the knowing does so without causal or passive dependence on that act. The remaining aseity–passivity question is then the separate question the literature on eternalist reconciliations already debates, and the paper positions itself against that literature rather than against Molinist sources. The libertarian open-options condition (W4220703499.c006) is kept as a separate requirement on the created agent, not used to ground divine knowledge. The paper does not claim that eternal presence itself grounds answerability through a new relation, and it does not rely on divine simplicity or timeless vision to do so.

**How the paper answers the main objection:** The paper separates two senses of 'grounded': causal or passive dependence bearing on aseity, and content-dependence, where the fact known fixes the correctness conditions of the knowing. The objection slides from the first to the second. The paper concedes content-dependence, as the Grounding Principle already does, and denies causal or passive dependence. Counterfactual covariation of content with fact is the mark of factive knowledge, not of a mere disposition. The paper's criterion is that a state is knowledge of p when its correctness is fixed by p and it would be assessed incorrect were p otherwise. Eternal knowledge of Maren's noon act meets this criterion. The 'content varies while the knowing does not' claim is withdrawn as incoherent.
