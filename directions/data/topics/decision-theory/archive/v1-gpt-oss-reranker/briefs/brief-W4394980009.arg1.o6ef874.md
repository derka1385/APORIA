## Revised after the quality check
*The revision narrows c058 from 'no certainty and hope' to 'no certainty of the wager's determinate object and no object-directed hope', separates determinacy from existence, and supplies an argument that procedural certainty and immanent hope cannot meet the apologetic requirement.* The thesis was narrowed.

**Revised question:** What kind of certainty and hope must a version of Pascal's wager provide to be suitable for apologetic use, and does the postmodern rendition lack the determinacy of object that apologetics requires, once procedural certainty and immanent hope are granted?

A paper here would argue that the premise c058, read as 'the postmodern rendition gives the seeker no certainty and no hope', is false and should be withdrawn, because the Kael case shows procedural certainty about daily action and immanent hope in a forward arc. The defensible premise is narrower: the postmodern rendition does not give the seeker certainty about the determinate object of the wager, and it does not give hope directed at that object. The paper distinguishes two things the referee's objection runs together. The first is determinacy, certainty about what the wager is on (which good is promised, which commitments are being staked, what the stake is). The second is existence, certainty that the object is real. Pascal's wager is explicitly a decision under uncertainty about existence, so it does not supply existence-certainty, and the paper does not require it. c054 and c060 concern determinacy, and Pascal supplies determinacy in the remainder of the Pensees. The paper then argues that apologetics needs determinacy, because apologetic persuasion must direct the hearer toward a specified object whose identity can be assented to, worshipped, and tested against the hearer's life, not merely toward a course of action. Procedural certainty cannot supply this, for a structural reason: Kael's certainty about tomorrow is invariant under the truth or falsity of the narrative. If the language-of-care story were false, his visit to the widow would still be the same act with the same procedural clarity, so the certainty does not discriminate among candidate objects and cannot function as a reason to assent to one. Immanent hope has the same limit. It is hope in the continuation of his own project, conditional on his acts, whereas the hope apologetics offers is hope in a good whose reality is the wager's stake, as in the afterlife and theodicy promises. The paper therefore argues that Pascal's wager remains the preferable apologetic tool because it supplies determinacy about the object while leaving existence open, and that the postmodern rendition, though it yields real procedural certainty and hope, does not supply the determinacy that apologetic use needs.

**How the paper answers the main objection:** The referee is right that the object-directed restriction must not remove Pascal's advantage, and the paper avoids that by separating determinacy of the object (what it is) from certainty of its existence. Pascal's wager keeps existence open, so it passes the same test as the restricted premise, and the restriction does not prove too much. The paper argues that apologetics needs determinacy, because a persuasive call must specify an object to assent to, and that Pascal supplies this in the Pensees. It then gives the argument the referee asks for. Procedural certainty is invariant under the truth of the narrative: Kael's visit to the widow is equally clear and equally compelling if the story is false, so it cannot discriminate among candidate objects. Immanent hope is conditional on his own acts and does not point to a determinate good beyond them. The Kael case is therefore granted as a real counterexample to the literal premise, and the premise is narrowed to what survives it.
