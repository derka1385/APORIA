**Revised question:** Does the paradigm of Christian faith require belief held with assurance or certitude as a constitutive component, or can faith that operates as assumption without belief be genuinely Christian, if deficient?

A paper here would argue that premise c030 should be restricted rather than rejected, and that the restriction changes what the Sarah case can show. The paper first fixes an independent criterion for a doxastic attitude: a subject believes p only if p functions for her as settled-true in theoretical and practical reasoning, and she revises p in belief's characteristic way when given counterevidence, treating not-p as a reason for surprise or inquiry. Self-report is evidence for this profile but does not settle it. An attitude adopted as a working assumption, used to guide practice without functioning as settled-true in theoretical reasoning, is non-doxastic by this criterion. Sarah's 'I trust, but I cannot claim to know' disclaims knowledge and certitude. Whether it also disclaims belief is a question the criterion answers, and the paper does not presuppose the answer. The paper then separates two readings of c030's 'too weak'. On the constitutive reading, any faith lacking a belief component is not faith at all. On the paradigm-normative reading, assurance and belief mark the standard against which faith is assessed, and a faith lacking them is deficient but still faith. The paper argues that the constitutive reading is refuted by any case that meets the non-doxastic criterion, and that the paradigm claim does not license it. The paradigm-normative reading survives, and it is the only reading on which the bishop's verdict of genuine but deficient faith is coherent. The thesis is therefore that c030 is false as a necessary-condition claim and true only as a claim about the paradigm, with the Sarah case refuting the first and leaving the second intact. The paper then engages the existing non-doxastic faith literature the referee identifies, to show what the criterion adds beyond the thesis that assurance is an ideal rather than a necessary condition. Its added content is a test that separates non-doxastic assumption from overbelief without knowledge, which the Jamesian model must pass to remain a rival account. If Sarah fails the test and is overbelieving, her case is compatible with c030, and the paper says so and rests on the paradigm reading.
