## Objection
*blind_thought_experimenter · qwen:Qwen/Qwen3.8-27B*

Consider Dr. Vasquez choosing between two treatments. Treatment A guarantees 50 years of healthy life. Treatment B offers 60% chance of 100 years and 40% chance of zero. EU ranks B above A cleanly. Now generalize: Treatment C guarantees eternal life (utility omega). Treatment D offers 75% chance of eternal life and 25% chance of zero. In surreal arithmetic, 0.75 times omega equals omega, so EU(C) equals EU(D). The generalization yields an indeterminate ranking. But this is not a paradox. The finite case already contains the identical structural feature: a 100% chance of 60 and a 100% chance of 60 are indistinguishable under EU, yet no one calls that a paradox. The label smuggles in an extra demand that EU must distinguish certain infinity from probabilistic infinity, a demand the finite framework never makes. The generalization reproduces the finite structure faithfully. No contradiction, no absurdity, no genuine paradox emerges. Vasquez simply faces a representational limitation identical to one she already accepts in the finite case.

**Revised question:** Given surreal-valued outcomes, which of the continuity (Archimedean) and dominance-style principles can a surreal expected-utility ranking satisfy when probabilities are restricted to the reals, and does satisfying them require admitting infinitesimal probabilities?

A paper here would argue that surreal expected utility ranks sure infinite payoffs and their probabilistic counterparts correctly, so the earlier tie claim is withdrawn. The real pressure point is continuity. With real probabilities, any positive chance q of an infinite outcome omega exceeds the sure finite outcome 1, since q·omega is still infinite and therefore greater than 1, while 1 is strictly above 0. The Archimedean principle requires some real q with q·omega below 1 whenever omega is preferred to 1 and 1 to 0, and no such q exists, so the ranking violates continuity. The paper would show that this failure is a genuine structural cost of restricting probabilities to the reals, not an artifact of the arithmetic, and it would compare two repairs: admitting infinitesimal probabilities (which restores the Archimedean condition and is the choice that has to be justified), or keeping real probabilities and accepting the discontinuity. It would engage Chen and Rubio's 'Surreal Decisions' directly, and the surrounding literature (Hajek, Bartha, Pivato), on which probability domains are admissible and what each choice commits the theorist to.
