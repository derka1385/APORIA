## Challenged premise
**W7203485685.c006**: Robust contrastive responsibility requires cognition that represents an outcome as issuing from the agent's own activity rather than relevant alternatives.

## Objection
*blind_thought_experimenter · openai:gpt-5.6-terra*

A control-room operator, Mira, selects lever A after reviewing two labeled simulations: A will divert a flood into an empty quarry; B will divert it into a village. She intends to save the village and pulls A. Her display represents only impersonal propositions: “A opens gate 1,” “gate 1 sends water to quarry,” and “B sends water to village.” It never represents the flood outcome as issuing from Mira’s own activity; it tracks lever states and consequences. The gate opens, the flood reaches the quarry, and the village is saved. Mira is robustly contrastively responsible for the quarry outcome rather than the village outcome: she knew the alternatives, deliberately selected the difference-making option, and would have pulled B had she intended the village outcome. Yet her cognition need not represent the outcome as issuing from her own activity rather than the alternative.

## Strongest responses and why they failed
- **Defender A**: Mira’s impersonal display does not exhaust her cognition: she represents a lever as the one she intends to pull and represents saving the village as her aim. This practical, intentional representation may supply the relevant self-involvement. — *Defender A grants that the case leaves open whether this is de se content about the outcome rather than merely a separately stored index concerning the act. The defender could not show that the outcome itself must be represented as issuing from the agent.*
- **Defender B**: The relevant requirement need not be an explicit thought such as “I cause the quarry outcome.” Mira represents A and B as options under her control, and her intention connects her selection of A to the outcome within an agential alternative-set. — *This reply narrows the premise to integrated agential representation rather than the stated requirement that the outcome be represented as issuing from the agent. It preserves a revised claim but concedes that c006 overstates what responsibility requires.*

## Open questions
- What distinguishes an integrated agential representation of an option and its outcome from impersonal causal tracking supplemented by a representation of an intended act?
- Does representing an act as available to oneself or intended by oneself require irreducible de se content?
- Must robust responsibility involve an outcome-directed source attribution, or is representation of one’s controlled act sufficient?
- Can the revised premise support the argument’s conclusion that divine cognition must include irreducible de se content?

## Paper direction
A paper here would argue that the move from robust contrastive responsibility to irreducible de se divine cognition depends on a stronger and presently unestablished source-attribution requirement. It would use cases such as Mira’s to distinguish impersonal representation of causal consequences, self-representation of an intended act, and representation of an outcome as attributable to one’s control; it would then assess whether only the last is necessary for responsibility. The strongest reply to anticipate is that practical deliberation already integrates agent, option, and outcome in a way that grounds attribution without explicit self-ascription. The paper would need to show either that this integration necessarily has irreducible de se content, or that responsibility can be grounded by act-level self-representation without the outcome-level de se representation required by the original argument.

## Revised after the quality check
*The thesis is narrowed from an outcome-level, case-based refutation of c006 to a conditional claim that the inference to irreducible de se divine cognition depends on whether act-level self-representation, which the Mira case already contains, is irreducibly de se.* The thesis was narrowed.

**Revised question:** Does robust contrastive responsibility require the agent's cognition to contain a representation of the selected outcome, or of the agent's act, as the agent's own, and if an act-level self-representation is what is required, is that content irreducibly de se, so that the inference to irreducible de se divine cognition holds?

A paper here would argue that the inference from robust contrastive responsibility to irreducible de se divine cognition goes through only if the source-attribution requirement is located at the level of the agent's intended act and that act-level content is irreducibly first-personal. The Mira case cannot show that outcome-level self-representation is unnecessary, because the intention she is stipulated to have already represents the village outcome as to be brought about by her pulling A. The paper therefore drops the claim that the case refutes c006 and instead proves a conditional. It distinguishes three kinds of content: impersonal representation of causal consequences (A opens gate 1; gate 1 sends water to the quarry), act-level self-representation carried by an intention to pull A, and outcome-level representation of the outcome as issuing from one's control. It then argues that robust responsibility requires the second kind whenever the selection is deliberate, and that the impersonal display contributes only the contrastive knowledge. If the act-level content is reducible to impersonal content, c052 fails, since the required self-involvement can be fully specified without de se content. If it is irreducible, c052 survives and the paper shows that the divine case turns on whether God's act of actualising, which is identical to the outcome, admits act-level de se content. The irreducibility question is therefore the paper's central target, and it is addressed directly against the accounts of de se content in the literature (Perry, Lewis, Cappelen–Dever), not assumed.

**How the paper answers the main objection:** The referee is right that Mira's intention already supplies self-involvement, so the case cannot serve as a counterexample to c006 as stated. The paper accepts this and uses the case to separate three contents: impersonal causal representation, act-level self-representation carried by intention, and outcome-level attribution. The impersonal display does not secure responsibility, because the contrastive knowledge in it is available without any attribution. The paper then argues that act-level self-representation is what a deliberate, contrastive selection requires, which narrows c006 from outcome-level to act-level. Against the charge that this leaves c052 untouched, the paper shows that the conclusion's strength depends entirely on whether act-level content is irreducibly de se, and it tests that claim against reductive proposals rather than assuming it. It also considers a case with no intention, to check whether any robust responsibility lacks first-personal content.
