## Challenged premise

## Objection
*blind_thought_experimenter · openai:gpt-5.6-terra*

Mira loves her estranged adult son, Leon, who has repeatedly said he wants reconciliation but is ashamed to initiate contact. Mira knows Leon’s current address and sends a plain letter: “I want a relationship with you; call me anytime.” She does not independently prove a general obligation that vulnerable parents must make reconciliation accessible; such a proof would require settling every possible family circumstance. Yet in this case, the letter successfully shows that Mira’s vulnerability is compatible with, and helps explain, her making access available: she risks rejection precisely because love makes Leon’s response matter. The analogy can therefore support the relevant claim about vulnerable divine love without first establishing an independent obligation to provide access.

## Revised after the quality check
*The revision replaces the claim that the Mira analogy supports obligation without a general principle with an explicit grounds-based bridge principle that is itself the local obligation, and narrows the universal claim to nonresistant persons in whom those grounds obtain.* The thesis was narrowed.

**Revised question:** Can a grounds-based account of when withholding access from a beloved is a failure of care, drawn from particular vulnerable loving relationships, establish a context-sensitive duty for a perfectly loving God to make relationship accessible to nonresistant persons, and does that duty hold for every such person?

A paper here would argue that vulnerable human love generates a duty to make relationship accessible when specified local grounds obtain: the beloved has expressed willingness to relate, cannot initiate contact for reasons other than their own resistance, and the lover can provide safe and available access. It would defend this grounds-based principle as an explicit bridge principle that is itself the context-sensitive obligation the analogical route requires, rather than a permission inferred from the Mira case, and it would argue that the Mira case illustrates the principle without establishing it on its own. The paper would then argue that these grounds can hold in the divine case for each nonresistant person, because they concern present access to relationship, which a reply appealing to eventual encounter does not supply, and because a perfectly knowing lover can assess each person's situation without a further general proof covering all circumstances. The paper would state its conclusion in narrowed form: the analogical route establishes a duty for every nonresistant person in whom the grounds obtain. Whether the grounds obtain universally, and whether permanent nonresistant nonbelief is compatible with perfect vulnerable love, are the points on which the universal form of P1 stands or falls, and the paper would identify them as such rather than assume them.

**How the paper answers the main objection:** The referee is right that the Mira case as first stated shows compatibility and explanation, which is permission-level, and the paper concedes that the case alone does not prove obligation. It replies by making the bridge principle explicit: when a lover knows the beloved's expressed willingness, the beloved's inability to initiate for reasons other than resistance, and that access can be provided safely, withholding it is a failure of care. The paper argues that this principle is a context-sensitive obligation, not a general one, and that its grounds are what generate the duty; the Mira case illustrates it. For universality, the paper argues that the grounds are present-tense conditions that a perfectly knowing God can assess for each person, so no general proof covering every circumstance is needed, and it rejects eventual encounter as a substitute because it does not satisfy the present-access ground. Where the grounds fail, the paper makes no claim.
