## Challenged premise
**W7212186029.c013**: Instantiation can be modelled as parthood because objects may be understood as mereological fusions of their properties.

## Objection
*blind_thought_experimenter · anthropic:sonnet*

Take a lamp, L, that is switched on at noon and off at one. Its property of being lit is instantiated at noon and not at one. Model instantiation as parthood: L is the fusion of its properties, so 'being lit' is a part of L. Now the lamp is switched off at one, and nothing in L's identity changes, yet the fusion has lost a part. But the fusion of its properties is supposed to be fixed by which properties it has, so the lamp at one o'clock is a different fusion from the lamp at noon, though it is the same lamp. Worse, the lamp also has the property of being a lamp, and that property is shared with every other lamp. A fusion of properties would then overlap with the fusion of a neighbouring lamp, so distinct objects share parts they should not share. Parthood cannot carry instantiation without either losing the lamp's persistence or making distinct objects overlap.

## Strongest responses and why they failed
- **Defender A**: The unrestricted premise fails for ordinary objects, but the divine case can be isolated: God's relevant properties are timeless and essential, so changes in instantiated properties do not threaten divine persistence. The overlap of shared properties is already accepted by the similarity-based framework. — *This response concedes that the challenged premise, as stated, is false or at least too broad, so it requires revision of the published argument. It reaches a restricted divine application but does not establish that timeless essential properties can be fusions or parts in the required sense.*
- **Defender B**: The lamp case need not imply numerical replacement if the relevant relata are time-indexed property instances rather than one timeless fusion of universals. Shared properties need not be objectionable, since distinct fusions can overlap and the framework permits non-antisymmetric parthood. — *Time-indexing answers the immediate persistence inference only by changing the proposed model from a fusion of an object's properties to an association with temporally indexed instances. It leaves open whether this revised relation is sufficiently unified, and whether its permissive overlap supports rather than weakens the intended account of instantiation.*

A paper here would argue that the parthood model of instantiation fails in the one domain where the divine-simplicity argument needs it, so the symmetry formulation can be stated but not grounded as a claim about instantiation. The paper drops the lamp-change case. Its target is the timeless divine case. It takes the premise under discussion in its restricted form: for God's essential properties, instantiation may be modelled as parthood. It then tests whether similarity-based parthood, as defined so that proper parthood is irreflexive, asymmetric and transitive for ordinary objects, and as permitting mutual parthood between God and omniscience while they remain numerically distinct, still expresses instantiation. Instantiation is directed: God is the bearer of omniscience, and not conversely. Mutual parthood makes the relation symmetric on the pair. The paper argues that the model therefore delivers co-characterization, a symmetric relation of shared characterization, unless it adds a direction-marking condition. Any such condition is a new primitive, and the paper shows that adding it either reintroduces the asymmetry that the symmetry formulation was meant to deny or leaves the relation without a principled ground. The paper also tests a second horn: whether defining proper parthood through similarity presupposes the simplicity thesis, because treating God's attributes as parts is only a substantive claim if the similarity relation is specified independently of the claim that God has no proper parts. The paper engages Paul's mereological bundle theory as the strongest version of the unrestricted view. It grants that Paul's acceptance of overlap through shared universals and the standard replies to change are coherent for ordinary objects. Its claim is that these replies do not transfer to God, since in the divine case the problem is directionality and not persistence. The paper concludes that the symmetry formulation is coherent as a formal thesis, but that the argument for it from instantiation-as-parthood is unsound unless the directionality problem is solved independently of the simplicity thesis.

**How the paper answers the main objection:** The referee is right that the lamp case does not touch the timeless divine application, so the paper drops it and presses the objection at the point where it bites in that case. The paper uses one distinction: instantiation is directed, while the mutual-parthood clause of the similarity model is symmetric. The argument for the symmetry formulation needs a relation that holds from God to God's parts and fails in the reverse direction, and the model only supplies a relation that can hold both ways. The paper replies that bundle-theoretic responses to change and overlap do not address this, because they concern persistence and identity, not the direction of predication. It then shows that adding a direction-marking condition either reintroduces asymmetry the formulation was meant to avoid or depends on the simplicity thesis for its specification. The paper therefore does not rely on the unrestricted premise the referee identifies as too broad.
