## Challenged premise
**W3208436359.c010**: A decision theory framed entirely in Conway’s surreal numbers serves as a conservative extension of finitist EU theory.

## Objection
*blind_thought_experimenter · anthropic:sonnet*

Ann must choose between Gamble X and Gamble Y. Both pay only finite amounts, and both have finite expected utility of exactly 5. X pays 5 for certain. Y pays 5 with probability 1, plus, with probability 1/ω (an infinitesimal), an additional payoff of 1 to the agent. Finitist EU theory counts the two options as tied, so it permits Ann to pick either. Every payoff and probability she faces is either finite or infinitesimal, and all of her expected utilities are finite reals. A surreal-framed theory computes Y's expectation as 5 + 1/ω, so it ranks Y strictly above X. That verdict compares two options whose finitist expectations are both 5, so the surreal theory issues a strict preference the finitist theory does not, and the extension is not conservative over finitist decision claims.

## Strongest responses and why they failed
- **Defender A**: Conservativeness is a claim only about the finite fragment of decision problems whose payoffs and probabilities are finite reals; Ann's gamble contains an infinitesimal probability, so it lies outside the fragment the finitist theory can express, and the finitist tie is merely a verdict about the truncated problem. A conservative extension may add strict preferences exactly where the base theory is silent. — *The objector accepted that this restricted reading preserves agreement on finite-valued problems but showed it renders the conservativeness claim too weak to support the foundation claim that surreal numbers provide a firm foundation for transfinite decision theory; Defender A conceded that this reading cannot carry the conclusion and that an additional normative premise is needed to warrant surreal strict preferences over infinitesimal-probability options.*
- **Defender B**: The finitist tie is an artifact of finitist theory's inability to register infinitesimal probabilities, not a judgment about a shared gamble; the surreal theory preserves every finitist preference among properly finitist gambles, and its strict ranking of Y over X is a feature that captures distinctions finitist theory must collapse, which is precisely why transfinite decision theory needs a richer number system. — *The objector replied that this restricts conservativeness to finite state spaces, leaving the premise too weak to support the foundation claim, and that recharacterizing a finitist permissive verdict as an expressive collapse does not demonstrate preservation of finitist verdicts; Defender B offered no valid close.*

## Open questions
- Is a finitist tie on a truncated expectation a genuine verdict that any extension must preserve, or merely an expressive silence that a richer theory may override?
- What normative principle could justify strict preferences in surreal decision theory when the expected utilities differ only by an infinitesimal amount?
- Can a decision theory count as a conservative extension of finitist EU theory if it issues strict preferences on gambles that finitist theory treats as permissively tied?

## Paper direction
A paper here would argue that conservativeness over the finite fragment of expected utility theory is insufficient to justify surreal numbers as a foundation for transfinite decision theory, because the surreal theory's strict preferences over infinitesimal-probability gambles contradict finitist permissive verdicts without independent normative warrant. The case would focus on the Ann gamble, in which both options have finite expectations and finitist theory permits either choice, whereas surreal theory strictly prefers the option with an infinitesimally higher expectation. The strongest reply to anticipate is that finitist theory cannot express infinitesimal probabilities, so its tie is merely an artifact of expressive limitation rather than a verdict that a conservative extension must preserve. The paper would need to show that either this finitist permissiveness constitutes a binding constraint on any legitimate extension, or that surreal decision theory can supply a non-question-begging normative argument for why infinitesimally superior expectations should ground strict preference.

*The revision drops the claim that c010 is false, which the out-of-language point defeats, and redirects the thesis to the bridge premise, using weak dominance as the warrant the paper must evaluate.* The thesis was narrowed.

**Revised question:** Does conservativeness over the finitist base language suffice to make Conway's surreal numbers a firm foundation for transfinite decision theory, or must the surreal theory independently warrant the verdicts it reaches only in its enriched language, such as strict preference over infinitesimal-probability gambles?

A paper here would argue that conservativeness over the finitist base language does not, by itself, establish surreal numbers as a firm foundation for transfinite decision theory. The Ann case cannot refute the conservativeness claim as stated (c010), because finitist EU takes only real-valued probabilities as inputs, so the 'finitist tie' between X and Y is produced by rounding 1/omega to 0, a step outside the base theory. The paper therefore drops the claim that c010 is false and narrows its target to the unstated bridge premise (arg1.mp): that conservativeness plus expressive enrichment yields a firm foundation. It argues that the bridge fails because the enriched verdicts, including Y's strict preference over X, need warrant of their own. The paper takes the referee's proposed warrant seriously, namely weak dominance, which Y satisfies state by state. It then tests whether weak dominance can carry that burden. The test engages Chen & Rubio's treatment of infinitesimal probabilities, lexicographic expected utility (Hausner, Fishburn, Blume-Brandenburger-Dekel), and the regularity debate (Easwaran, Pruss, Williamson). The paper examines whether dominance-based strict preference conflicts with other surreal verdicts, and whether the regularity and lexicographic literatures that motivate infinitesimals already supply a non-question-begging principle or merely relocate the dispute. The paper's contribution is to show that foundational status requires an account of enriched verdicts beyond conservativeness, and to identify what that account must contain. It does not claim that surreal decision theory is refuted.

**How the paper answers the main objection:** The paper concedes the out-of-language point. Conservativeness quantifies over sentences the base theory can express, and the Ann case is a problem with infinitesimal probabilities, which finitist EU cannot state, so the case does not falsify c010. The paper's reply is a distinction between base-language agreement and enriched-language verdicts. It accepts that the surreal theory is conservative on finite problems and holds that the bridge premise, which would move from that conservativeness to foundational status, is the claim that needs independent support. On the demand for warrant, the paper grants that weak dominance is the natural candidate. It argues that dominance is a necessary component of the warrant and that the paper must show whether it is sufficient, through its interaction with the other surreal verdicts and with the lexicographic and regularity positions.
