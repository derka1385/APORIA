## Challenged premise
**W7212186029.c004**: The symmetry formulation is viable as a thesis about God’s relation to his attributes because instantiation can plausibly be modeled as parthood.

## Objection
*reasoner:explorer · anthropic:sonnet*

Take a case where instantiation is modeled as parthood and see what breaks. Let a red sphere s instantiate redness r and roundness o. Model instantiation as parthood: r and o are parts of s. Now consider a bare particular b that instantiates nothing. Under the model, b has no parts. Consider a second sphere t that is red and round, and let s and t share both properties. Parthood as a relation among concrete things is transitive and, in standard mereologies, supports fusion: the fusion of r and o would be a part of s, and so would any fusion of attributes. But instantiation is not closed under fusion: a fusion of redness and roundness does not instantiate 'being red-and-round' as a single attribute that s bears in the same way. Worse, if s is a part of its own attribute-fusion, the model forces s to have itself as a proper part in a loop whenever an attribute is predicated of the attribute's bearer, violating the asymmetry that the symmetry formulation was meant to preserve. The modeling therefore cannot be both transitive and asymmetric, so the viability premise's antecedent fails in exactly the mereological framework the conclusion relies on.

## Strongest responses and why they failed
- **Defender A**: Defender A proposed that God and each attribute stand in mutual (symmetric) parthood, so that neither is a proper part and God therefore lacks non-symmetric parts. — *This escaped the objector's dilemma only by introducing a new premise not found in the original argument, and it incurs the cost of collapsing the distinction between God and his attributes at the level of parts while leaving the necessity worry unresolved.*
- **Defender B**: Defender B replied that the viability claim concerns only God's necessary attributes and that the argument does not endorse unrestricted fusion or symmetric parthood for ordinary objects. — *This failed because restricting the scope to necessary attributes does not block the core problem: if properties are non-symmetric parts of their bearers, God's attributes remain non-symmetric parts of God, contradicting the symmetry formulation unless one adopts mutual parthood.*

## Open questions
- Does mutual parthood between God and his attributes collapse the metaphysical distinction between God and each attribute, and is this collapse compatible with classical theism?
- Can the necessity worry—that necessary properties entail necessary parthood facts—be resolved without making God's attributes exist in all possible worlds exactly as God does?
- Is there an alternative mereology of instantiation that treats properties as parts of bearers without forcing either non-symmetric parthood or mutual parthood?

## Paper direction
A paper here would argue that the symmetry formulation of divine simplicity cannot be vindicated by modeling instantiation as standard non-symmetric parthood, because constituent ontology makes God's attributes non-symmetric parts of God, directly contradicting the thesis that God lacks such parts. The case would trace the objector's dilemma that any one-directional parthood relation either makes God the whole of which attributes are parts or else ceases to be parthood in the framework's intended sense, and it would demonstrate that the only viable repair is to adopt mutual parthood between God and his attributes. The strongest reply to anticipate is that mutual parthood preserves the letter of the symmetry formulation by eliminating proper-parthood loops, while restricting the model to God's necessary attributes avoids overgeneralization. What the paper would need to show is that this mutual-parthood repair is not merely a technical fix but a philosophically coherent account of how God relates to his attributes: it must establish that mutual parthood does not collapse God and his attributes into indistinguishable mereological fusions, and it must resolve the necessity worry by explaining how symmetric parthood can hold without making God's attributes modally on a par with God himself.

A paper here would argue that the symmetry formulation of divine simplicity is not secured by the bare claim that instantiation is parthood, but only by a restricted and specified parthood relation. The paper drops the earlier claim that non-symmetric parthood contradicts the doctrine and instead treats mutual parthood between God and his necessary attributes as the content of the symmetry formulation itself. Its contestable target is premise c004 and its implicit step arg1.mp. The paper will argue in two parts. First, it will show that the transitivity and fusion-closure principles of standard mereology cannot hold for instantiation, because instantiation is not closed under fusion, so the model must give up unrestricted composition. Second, it will argue that the restriction to necessary attributes is principled rather than ad hoc: the only parthood relation that can carry the symmetry formulation is one whose composition principles are limited to the necessary attributes of a single bearer. To make this claim precise, the paper will state the necessity worry as follows. If each necessary attribute of God is a part of God and God is a part of that attribute, then the mutual parthood holds necessarily, which threatens to make the attributes modally on a par with God. The paper will define a collapse criterion, namely that God and an attribute are collapsed when every mereological and modal predicate true of one is true of the other, and will argue that mutual parthood without antisymmetry or extensionality does not meet this criterion because the relevant parthood is not extensional. The paper will also argue that denying antisymmetry for God is an extra commitment that must be justified independently of the doctrine, and it will assess whether such denial can be motivated without circularity. The paper does not claim that instantiation is parthood in general, and it does not defend the symmetry formulation against non-symmetric formulations on grounds of mere consistency.
