## Challenged premise
**W7172336769.c010**: The analogical framework introduces a further distinction between res significata and modus significandi, providing the resources needed to ground the meaning of theological predicates.

## Objection
*reasoner:formalist · kimi:moonshotai/Kimi-K2.6*

The premise assumes that drawing a line between res significata and modus significandi automatically yields the semantic resources to ground theological predicates. But this is a formal distinction about how language operates, not a substantive account of what the divine res significata contains. Consider the predicate 'good': the distinction tells us that the mode of signifying is creaturely, and that God is the thing signified. It gives us no purchase on what God's goodness consists in; it merely labels the gap between human concept and divine reality. The analogical framework denies that the modus applies to God, and the res in its divine mode remains unknown, so the distinction leaves the content of 'good' as applied to God semantically underdetermined. The inference from introducing this distinction to providing resources to ground meaning therefore misses a step: classifying the structure of signification is not the same as determining its content. The premise treats a map of semantic relations as if it were the territory of semantic determination.

## Strongest responses and why they failed
- **Defender A**: The argument does not require the distinction to reveal what God's goodness consists in; rather, the analogical framework supplies constraints and a fixed relation to creatures so that 'good' is admitted because the res signified is truly in God while the modus is creaturely, which excludes ill-formed predications, with determinacy construed as admissibility and truth-conditions relative to a grounding relation rather than a positive inventory. — *The objector forced a concession that the bare res/modus distinction cannot determine positive content, so Defender A had to restrict the determinacy claim to admissibility constraints and weaken the argument's conclusion accordingly.*
- **Defender B**: The distinction does not operate alone but is situated within a broader semantic-metaphysical framework in which a fixed metaphysical relation between God and creatures supplies the content of analogical predicates, while the distinction merely articulates the structural difference between creaturely mode and divine res. — *It preserved the argument by showing the distinction operates in tandem with metaphysical grounding, but it had to concede that the original premise's phrasing overclaims by suggesting the formal distinction itself suffices, leaving unresolved how the metaphysical relation fixes determinate content without an independent theory of meaning.*
