## Objection
*reasoner:skeptic · glm:zai-org/GLM-5.2*

Consider a critic who argues that divine simplicity is incoherent because 'God is wise' and 'God is good' seem to predicate distinct attributes of one simple being. A defender responds using only Frege's sense/reference distinction: both sentences share the same reference (God himself) while differing in sense, much as 'the morning star is bright' and 'the evening star is bright' differ in sense but co-refer. This directly defuses the critic's worry that distinct predicates imply distinct properties in God. The response is complete without invoking analogy, proportionality, or any analogical framework. The critic's concern—that simplicity threatens coherent predication—is addressed purely by the Fregean diagnostic that sameness of reference is compatible with difference of sense. The premise claims Fregean diagnostics MUST be located within the doctrine of analogy to address such concerns, but this case shows the concerns are already addressable without that framework.

## Paper direction
A paper here would argue that the defense of divine simplicity overstates the relationship between Fregean semantics and Thomistic analogy by claiming that Fregean diagnostics must be located within the analogical framework, when in fact the Fregean sense/reference distinction independently resolves the identity worry that distinct predicates imply distinct properties in a simple God, while analogy separately supplies determinate content and admissibility constraints. The paper would show that the argument survives only if its central premise is revised from structural subordination to conditional dependence, so that Fregean diagnostics are deemed insufficient for the determinacy concern without analogy rather than embedded within it. The strongest reply to anticipate is that without subordinating Fregean tools to analogy, the two frameworks yield a disjointed semantics in which formal coherence is purchased at the cost of unified metaphysical grounding. To succeed, the paper would need to demonstrate that conditional dependence still leaves analogy as the metaphysically prior framework for fixing which senses are admissible for God, while conceding that the identity worry requires no analogical embedding.

*This is a sharper and better-aimed proposal than the original objection, but it currently rests on a shaky reading of Frege and ignores the obvious purely Fregean nominalization reply, so it is not yet a defensible paper.*

- Strongest remaining objection: The paper's pivotal claim misstates Frege and ignores a standard purely Fregean move. On Frege's semantics, predicates express senses and refer to concepts, and the identity-analogue for concepts is coextension, so distinct senses do not entail distinct concept-referents. More importantly, defenders since Stump and Kretzmann routinely nominalize the attributes ('God's wisdom', 'God's goodness'). These are co-referring singular terms that differ in sense and pick out the divine essence, which is exactly a 'purely Fregean reassignment' requiring no analogical semantics. The thesis that analogy is needed to license the reassignment is therefore unmotivated. It also sits poorly with Aquinas, for whom the res significata is the perfection itself and the modus significandi is the creaturely mode, not a Fregean sense.
- What it still needs: The paper must engage the nominalization and coextension strategies, together with truthmaker reconstruals of simplicity (Bergmann and Brower), and show why each fails to secure attribute identity without analogical resources. It must also give a textually defensible mapping from res significata, ratio and modus significandi onto Fregean sense and reference instead of simply asserting one.
