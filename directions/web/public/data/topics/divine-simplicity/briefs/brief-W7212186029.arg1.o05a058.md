## Challenged premise
**W7212186029.arg1.r1**: Instantiation, modeled as parthood, holds symmetrically between God and each of his attributes, so that God is part of each attribute as well as each attribute part of God.

## Objection
*reasoner:explorer · kimi:moonshotai/Kimi-K2.6*

Consider a fire that instantiates heat. On a mereological model of instantiation, heat is an aspectual part of the fire: it cannot exist apart from it and is a constituent of its state. Yet the fire is not part of the heat; the heat is an effect and dependent feature that does not encompass its source. If God and his attributes are modeled on this schema—God as the fire, his goodness as the warmth—then parthood tracks instantiation downward only. The attribute is part of God, but God is not part of the attribute. The supposed symmetry vanishes.

## Open questions
- Can a symmetric grounding relation between God and his attributes be given independent metaphysical motivation, or does it remain a formal artifact tailored to block proper parthood?
- Does the retreat to formal consistency alone undermine the philosophical significance of the symmetry formulation as an interpretation of divine simplicity?
- If instantiation is modeled as mutual parthood, what prevents the model from collapsing into an identity formulation or into an asymmetric dependence relation?

## Paper direction
A paper here would argue that the symmetric parthood formulation of divine simplicity survives the fire-heat objection only as a claim of formal consistency, and that its status as a philosophically viable doctrine depends on whether an independent metaphysical account of the God-attribute relation can be provided. The case would trace how the objection exposes the original premise as an undefended stipulation, and would evaluate whether non-causal grounding or intrinsic nature claims can supply the needed symmetry without collapsing into asymmetric dependence. The strongest reply to anticipate is that formal viability is all the conclusion claims, so no further metaphysical motivation is required. The paper would need to show either that this retreat leaves the symmetry formulation too thin to count as an interpretation of divine simplicity, or that a substantive metaphysics of mutual parthood for divine attributes can be developed and defended against standard asymmetric models of instantiation.
