## Challenged premise
**W2575351351.c017**: Goods individually less valuable than a relationship with God can collectively make a world containing nonresistant nonbelief more valuable.

## Objection
*blind_thought_experimenter · anthropic:sonnet*

Consider Mara, a widow who, through a period of nonresistant nonbelief, becomes unusually attentive to her neighbors. Her absence of belief in God means she does not pray, so she never receives the consolation or moral formation a relationship with God would have given her. Instead she gains three goods: she rebuilds a fractured friendship with her estranged brother, she learns patience through caring for a dying stranger, and she forms a community garden with people who would otherwise have been isolated. Each good is valued below a relationship with God. Yet the collective tally is supposed to exceed that relationship's value. But these three goods are not additive across her life: the friendship and the garden each draw on the same disposition of open-handed attention that a relationship with God would have cultivated more deeply and more reliably. Once the relationship is counted as a standing source of that disposition, the goods are not independent, and the sum she realizes is worth less than the single relationship she forwent.

## Strongest responses and why they failed
- **Defender A**: The relevant goods should be assessed net of overlap, and Mara's reconciliation, care relation, and garden may involve distinct recipients and relations rather than merely one disposition of attention. — *Defender A ultimately conceded that this qualification is absent from c017 as written. The case also does not establish that the remaining non-overlapping goods exceed a relationship valued for itself.*
- **Defender B**: The comparison should concern whole feasible histories: a relationship with God cultivating attention need not secure this particular package of reconciliation, reciprocal care, and mutual support without loss. The many-goods response therefore remains a possibility claim for bounded temporary nonbelief. — *The objector correctly pressed that a possibility claim still requires a non-duplicative package whose net value exceeds the lost relationship-value. Mara, as described, does not provide such a case, and Defender B accepted revision of the premise.*

## Open questions
- What account of value determines when goods sharing a dispositional source are genuinely additional rather than double-counted?
- What counterfactual standard establishes that a God-relationship alternative would not duplicate or guarantee a whole relational package?
- Can the intrinsic value of a relationship with God be outweighed by finite, jointly realized goods, and if so under what axiological assumptions?
- Are there plausible cases of bounded nonresistant nonbelief that yield distinctive relational goods not available within an alternative relationship with God?

## Paper direction
A paper here would argue that the many-goods response requires a net-of-overlap formulation and cannot rely on an unqualified aggregation of individually lesser goods. It would develop the case for comparing whole feasible histories, rather than merely counting named outcomes, while anticipating the strongest reply that shared dispositions and the intrinsic value of divine relationship make the goods non-additive or non-compensatory. To succeed, the paper would need to specify a defensible measure of non-duplicative value, defend the relevant counterfactual comparison with the God-relationship alternative, and identify a plausible bounded case in which the surviving package outweighs the lost relationship.

## Revised after the quality check
*The revision narrows the thesis to an existential possibility claim, drops net-of-overlap as a standalone thesis, and adds a counterfactual distinctiveness test, a commensurability argument, and a worked bounded case.*

**Revised question:** Can a bounded period of nonresistant nonbelief generate a set of goods that is distinctive to nonbelief, where no feasible history within the relationship with God realizes those goods, and whose combined value, net of shared dispositional sources, exceeds the intrinsic value of the lost relationship?

A paper here would argue for a bounded existential claim: there are feasible cases in which temporally limited nonresistant nonbelief yields several goods that are individually below the value of a relationship with God, that do not depend on one another's dispositional sources, and that together outweigh that relationship. The paper would not claim that most or typical cases succeed, and it would not defend the net-of-overlap formulation as a distinct thesis, since the referee is right that competent aggregation already assesses value net of overlap. Overlap instead becomes a constraint on case construction: a candidate case must show that its goods have separate sources. The paper would give three components. First, an axiological argument on commensurability: it would argue, against lexical priority of the relational good, that finite goods can in principle be compared with and outweigh a supreme relational good, and it would engage the existing axiological and greater-goods literature directly on this point. Second, a principled counterfactual test for distinctiveness: a good G counts as unavailable within the relationship only if every feasible history in which the agent is in that relationship and realizes G's core features requires an element the relationship cannot supply, such as the specific reconciliation that presupposes the prior estrangement. The claim that a relationship would cultivate the same disposition 'more deeply and more reliably' would be treated as a premise to be tested under this test, not assumed. Third, a worked bounded case with separate sources for each good, checked against the test, that shows the package outweighs the lost relationship under a stated value measure.

**How the paper answers the main objection:** The paper accepts that one overlapping case such as Mara's refutes only that instance, not the possibility claim in c017, so the modal thesis is kept as an existential claim. It answers the referee's concern about question-begging by replacing the assertion that a relationship would cultivate the disposition more reliably with a counterfactual test: a good is distinctive only if its core features are unrealizable within the relationship across all feasible histories. The paper then supplies a candidate case whose goods have independent sources, so the test, not the critic's assertion, decides overlap. It also drops net-of-overlap as a separate thesis, conceding that it is implicit in aggregation, and it argues directly for commensurability, which is the substantive question the referee identifies.

**Re-assessment** (fresh read, the assessor did not see its earlier critique): **needs work** · overall 3.5/5 · coherence 4/5 · robustness 3/5 · significance 3/5 · specificity 4/5

*A writable and respectable refinement of the many-goods reply, but it will stand or fall on whether the distinctiveness test can avoid trivial fine-grained individuation and whether the value comparison is set up as bounded delay rather than total loss.*

- Strongest remaining objection: The distinctiveness test faces an individuation dilemma. If goods are individuated finely, as in 'this reconciliation, which presupposes this estrangement,' they trivially pass the test, because any history-indexed token is unrealizable in a different history. But then their value is not plausibly indexed to the token, and a value-relevantly equivalent type of good (reconciliation, forgiveness, patience in care) is available within the relationship. If goods are individuated by value-relevant type, they will almost always fail the test. Either way, the worked case outweighs the relationship only because of an individuation choice and a stipulated value measure, which a hiddenness theorist will simply reject.
- What it still needs: It must give a principled, non-stipulative account of the 'core features' that fix a good's identity for value purposes, one that makes distinctiveness non-trivial without collapsing it. It must also frame the comparison correctly as worlds or histories with a bounded interval of lost relationship, not as package versus the whole intrinsic value of the relationship, and pair this with a commensurability argument that engages lexical-priority views rather than assuming them away.
