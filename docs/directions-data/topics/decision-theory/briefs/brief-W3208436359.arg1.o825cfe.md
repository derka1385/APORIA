Yet the administrator recognizes no genuine extra value: the infinitesimal term is an artifact of an unreachable infinite stage, not an actual benefit to anyone.

## Open questions
- Can the surreal framework be supplemented with a principled criterion that distinguishes realizable infinitesimal benefits from mere formal artifacts?
- Does preserving dominance and completeness in an extended ordered field provide sufficient normative warrant for strict preferences that have no experiential correlate within finite time?
- Is the hospital administrator's refusal to prefer protocol Y a legitimate normative constraint that any transfinite decision theory must respect, or merely a finitist bias to be overcome?
- How should a foundation for transfinite decision theory adjudicate between formal representability and outcome-grounded normative authority when the two diverge?

## Paper direction
A paper here would argue that surreal-number expected utility theory faces a foundational dilemma exposed by the hospital case: its strict preferences over infinitesimally separated options either must be grounded in actual, realizable outcomes, in which case the surreal machinery itself offers no way to identify which differences qualify, or they must be justified by the structural demands of completeness and dominance in an extended ordered field, in which case the theory severs the link between preference and welfare. The paper would develop the case that ranking 5 + 1/ω above 5 when no finite patient experiences any extra benefit undermines the normative warrant for surreal strict preferences, and would anticipate the reply that preserving dominance and structural rationality suffices to justify the ranking. What the paper would need to show is that either structural coherence can genuinely normatively compel strict preference without any realizable difference, or that a principled, non-arbitrary criterion can be embedded within the surreal framework to filter out artifactual infinitesimal differences without collapsing the theory into finitism.

## Revised after the quality check
*The revision drops the hospital counterexample, which the referee showed rests on a modeling error and an undefined infinite sum, and relocates the challenge to infinitesimals generated by the theory's own probability arithmetic, where the input-dependence reply does not transfer from finite EU.* The thesis was narrowed.

**Revised question:** Can a surreal-number expected utility theory generate strict preferences from its own arithmetic, such as those arising from infinitesimal probabilities, that are normatively warranted when the utility inputs are fixed and shared across representations that agree on every finite outcome?

A paper here would argue that the hospital case fails as a counterexample to surreal expected utility. It stipulates an infinitesimal utility for an outcome the case describes as valueless, and it relies on an infinite sum that surreal arithmetic does not define, so the verdict reflects a modeling error rather than the framework. The paper concedes this and narrows its thesis to a case the framework generates on its own terms: a decision in which the infinitesimal difference arises from the theory's probability arithmetic, for example when two options differ only by an infinitesimal probability of a finite benefit within a mixed strategy, while all utility inputs are fixed and identical. The argument is that in such a case the surreal verdict is not determined by the inputs, because the same inputs and the same finite outcomes admit surreal, hyperreal, and lexicographic representations that rank the options differently or leave them tied. Finite expected utility theory has no analogue of this. Once utilities are fixed, its verdicts are fixed, so the reply that the input is at fault does not transfer. The paper therefore claims that a strict surreal preference in this case is not normatively warranted by value differences, since no realizable difference in outcomes grounds it and its presence depends on a representational choice the decision problem does not supply. It then tests whether a non-arbitrary realizability criterion, requiring that an infinitesimal probability difference correspond to a difference in some realizable outcome distribution, can be stated within the surreal framework without collapsing it into finitism. If no such criterion is available, the thesis is that surreal strict preferences over infinitesimally separated options are underdetermined by value and should not be treated as decisive.

**How the paper answers the main objection:** The paper concedes the hospital case: the referee is right that assigning 1/ω while denying any value is a modeling error, and that surreal arithmetic lacks general infinite sums, so that case is dropped rather than defended. The remaining worry is answered by distinguishing two sources of infinitesimal terms. In the hospital case the term is an input, so the garbage-in reply applies. In the mixed-strategy case the term is produced by the theory's own probability arithmetic from fixed, shared inputs. Here the finite analogy fails: finite EU verdicts are fixed once utilities are fixed, whereas the verdict between options differing only by an infinitesimal probability can vary across representations (surreal, hyperreal, lexicographic) that agree on every finite outcome and every input. The paper argues that a strict preference which varies with representation and tracks no realizable outcome difference is not warranted by value, and it assesses whether a realizability criterion can be stated without collapsing into finitism.
