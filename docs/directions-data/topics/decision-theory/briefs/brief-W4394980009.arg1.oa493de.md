## Paper direction

The case would trace the objector's counterexample of a seeker who finds ambiguity liberating, note the defenders' shared concession that the original premise c057 must be narrowed, and argue that this narrowing merely relocates the dispute rather than resolving it. The strongest reply to anticipate is that apologetics is definitionally oriented toward supplying grounds and hope to questioners, so excluding seekers who embrace ambiguity is not question-begging but a clarification of the practice's internal norms. What the paper would need to show is either that these internal norms are themselves contested by postmodern theologians who practice apologetics without prioritizing determinate certainty, or that Pascal's text, particularly when read through the lens of negative theology and the sublime, does not in fact provide the determinate object of certainty that the restricted argument claims to recover.
