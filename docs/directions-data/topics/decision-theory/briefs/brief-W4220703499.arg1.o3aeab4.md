## Challenged premise
**W4220703499.c025**: Positing God as eternal, with all time present to Him, best respects both divine foreknowledge and libertarian free will.

## Objection
*blind_thought_experimenter · glm:zai-org/GLM-5.2*

Sarah faces a choice at T2: she can help a stranger or walk past. God is eternal, with all moments present to Him, so God perceives Sarah walking past at T2—not as a prediction but as a present, completed reality within His timeless awareness. God's eternal perception is fixed and unalterable; it encompasses the entire timeline as one undivided whole. Now Sarah at T2 deliberates. For libertarian freedom, it must be within Sarah's power to help instead. But if Sarah were to help, God's eternal perception would have included her helping—which it does not. Since God's perception is eternally complete and cannot be otherwise, Sarah cannot do otherwise than what God perceives. The fixity is not removed; it is made permanent. Compare open theism: God knows all real possibilities but not which choice Sarah will make, leaving her future genuinely open. This alternative better respects libertarian freedom, since Sarah's choice remains undetermined by any prior or eternal fact.

## Open questions
- Does the addition of a logical-posteriority or cross-world contingency premise genuinely dissolve the modal fixity objection, or does it merely relocate the problem by treating God's eternal knowledge as a necessary consequence of an act that still appears fixed from the eternal perspective?
- Can the eternalist maintain that God's knowledge is immutable within any single possible world while also granting that the creature has the power to actualize a different world, without collapsing the distinction between God's knowledge and a necessary determinant of the act?
- Is the distinction between explanatory grounding and modal fixity stable when God's eternal perception presents every temporal moment as an undivided whole, or does the eternal perspective inevitably undermine the openness required by libertarian freedom?

## Paper direction
A paper here would argue that the modal fixity objection to eternalist reconciliations of divine foreknowledge and libertarian freedom can be answered by supplementing the Grounding Principle with a premise about the logical posteriority and cross-world contingency of God's eternal knowledge. The case would develop the defenders' revised claims—that God's eternal perception is grounded in the creature's choice and, though immutable within any single possible world, is contingent across possible worlds on that free choice—to show that eternal fixity is the consequence rather than the constraint of the creature's act. The strongest reply to anticipate is the objector's charge that grounding and logical priority are explanatory rather than modal relations, so that an eternally unalterable perception which necessarily entails the act still removes the power to do otherwise at the moment of choice. The paper would need to show that logical posteriority within God's eternal act genuinely leaves the creature's options open at T2 rather than merely redescribing a fixed eternal fact, and that the appeal to cross-world contingency does not presuppose the libertarian conclusion it is meant to defend.

**Revised question:** Can the timeless-knowledge transfer argument be blocked by denying its first premise, that God's eternal belief is fixed relative to a temporal agent like an accidentally necessary past fact, on independent grounds, or does eternal fixity remain incompatible with the power to do otherwise?

A paper here would argue that the transfer argument (Pike, Plantinga's 'On Ockham's Way Out', Zagzebski 1991, Hasker 1989) can be blocked by denying its first premise, that God's timeless belief is as fixed relative to Sarah at T2 as a past fact is. The paper abandons the earlier supplement of logical posteriority and cross-world contingency, because that move leaves premises (i) and (iii) untouched and so restates the Boethian and Anselmian position already held to be insufficient. The denial rests on the Grounding Principle (c007): the truth of God's belief depends on the actual choice, so the belief is not a hard fact that obtains independently of the agent's options at the time of choice. The paper defends this with a distinction between hard and soft facts. A soft fact is one whose unalterability relative to an agent is itself counterfactually dependent on that agent's act. Eternal beliefs are soft in this sense. The paper supports this with c019: the belief cannot be fixed at an earlier time, since S cannot be choosing before he is choosing. Rule Beta is granted in its proper modal scope, but it transfers necessity only from hard facts. The paper engages Rogers' Anselmian account on this point, using the open-options thesis (c006) as the test the soft-fact denial must meet. The conclusion is conditional: if the soft-fact denial can be independently motivated, eternalist reconciliation survives the transfer argument. If it cannot, the paper concedes that eternal fixity is incompatible with the power to do otherwise. Stump and Kretzmann, Leftow and Fischer serve as the standard rivals against which the soft-fact account is tested. The open-theist comparison is dropped, since open theism gives up exhaustive foreknowledge and cannot better respect both desiderata.

**How the paper answers the main objection:** The referee is right that cross-world contingency and posteriority concede premises (i) and (iii) and so do not answer the transfer argument. The paper therefore denies premise (i). It argues that God's timeless belief is a soft fact relative to Sarah at T2: its unalterability holds only given her act, and it inherits that status through the Grounding Principle (c007). Rule Beta is granted, but it transfers necessity only from hard facts that do not depend on the agent's options. Independent support comes from c019, which shows the belief cannot be fixed at a time before the choice. The paper's claim is that Sarah's power to help is a power over the act, whose counterfactual dependence includes the belief. It does not require that she now alter a fact independent of her act. Whether this is a motivated distinction or an ad hoc relabelling is the decisive test.

A belief held by an essentially infallible God is a real intrinsic state of God, and that state is fixed whatever its entailments are. Defining softness as 'unalterability counterfactually dependent on the agent's act' assumes the very thing in dispute, namely that Sarah could have acted otherwise. Unless the paper explains why God's eternal belief-state, as opposed to the proposition it entails, is not fixed relative to Sarah, the proposal just relabels the fact.
