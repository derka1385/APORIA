## Challenged premise
**W7212186029.c004**: The symmetry formulation is viable as a thesis about God’s relation to his attributes because instantiation can plausibly be modeled as parthood.

## Objection
*reasoner:formalist · kimi:moonshotai/Kimi-K2.6*

If God instantiates an attribute such as wisdom, modeling that instantiation as parthood makes wisdom a part of God, not God a part of wisdom. The symmetry formulation requires that God be a part of every part of God: whenever something is a part of God, God is also a part of it. The premise assumes that merely modeling instantiation as parthood yields this mutual parthood, but it only establishes a one-directional relation from attribute to substance. Without a further premise ensuring that God is likewise a part of each attribute, the symmetry formulation cannot get off the ground as a thesis about God's relation to his attributes.

## Paper direction
A paper here would argue that the symmetry formulation of divine simplicity cannot be derived from the mere claim that instantiation is modeled as parthood, because standard substance-attribute parthood is asymmetric and the formal mereological framework only permits, but does not entail, symmetric relations once antisymmetry is dropped. The case would examine the specific gap identified by the objector: modeling instantiation as parthood establishes at most that attributes are parts of God, whereas the symmetry formulation additionally requires that God be a part of each attribute. The strongest reply to anticipate is that the framework's rejection of antisymmetry makes symmetric parthood available and that divine attributes, as intrinsic parts of God's nature, are special cases where mutual parthood is plausible. To succeed, the paper would need to show either that there is an independent metaphysical argument for symmetric divine parthood that does not merely presuppose the doctrine's conclusion, or that the symmetry formulation, even if formally consistent, captures a genuinely distinct and theologically acceptable version of divine simplicity rather than a technical artifact of non-standard mereology.

**How the paper answers the main objection:** The objection is granted in part. Modeling instantiation as parthood yields attribute-to-God parthood only, so the earlier inference from the model to symmetric applicability fails. The paper replies by separating two claims that the original premise ran together. The first is formulability: the symmetry formulation is stated in a framework that permits symmetric parthood, so it is viable in the referee's sense of formulable and applicable. This is granted and needs no entailment from the model. The second is applicability to the God-attribute pair, which requires that God also be part of each attribute. The paper treats this as the doctrine's substantive commitment, to be argued for rather than derived. Against the referee's point that every formulation carries its own content, the paper accepts this and argues that the real question is whether that content is coherent and faithful.
