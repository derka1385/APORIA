## Objection
*hidden_premise_attacker · llama:nvidia/Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct-FP8*

Consider a scenario where God's volition to create the world is an intrinsic property, driven by God's own nature and desires. In this case, God's relation to the world is still partially based on facts extrinsic to God, such as the world's existence and properties. However, the accidental property of volition remains intrinsic to God, as it is a fundamental aspect of God's character. This scenario suggests that the connection between God's relation to the world and the nature of accidental properties is not as straightforward as assumed.

## Open questions
- Can a property that originates entirely from God's necessary nature nevertheless be extrinsic if its minimal truthmaker includes contingent objects?
- Does the object-based analysis of volition, on which the contingent world is the object rather than an intrinsic constituent, preserve divine simplicity when that object enters the truthmaker?
- Is the revised bridge premise—that the inclusion of contingent facts in the minimal truthmaker of God's volition suffices for its extrinsicality—an independent principle or merely a restatement of the conclusion?
- Are there coherent models of intrinsic accidental properties on which God's volition has only God as its minimal truthmaker despite the world's contingency?

## Paper direction
A paper here would argue that the modal-collapse objection to divine simplicity can be answered only by replacing the standard bridge from extrinsic relations to extrinsic properties with a direct truthmaker bridge: the contingent facts about the world enter the minimal truthmaker for God's accidental volitional and cognitive properties, and this inclusion alone renders those properties at least partially extrinsic regardless of whether they flow from God's necessary nature. The case would develop a rigorous truthmaker semantics for divine predication, show that the objector's scenario of an intrinsically-grounded volition collapses into an equivocation between metaphysical origins and truthmaker composition, and anticipate the strongest reply that the object-based route reintroduces complexity into God by making the world a constituent of the divine truthmaker. To succeed, the paper would need to show either that the object's presence in the truthmaker does not violate simplicity—perhaps by treating the object as a non-constituent truthmaker—or that an alternative truthmaker analysis can ground extrinsicality without including the world in God's being at all.

**Revised question:** Is the premise that extrinsic grounding of God's relation to the world makes God's volition and knowledge extrinsic valid as stated, and if not, can God's accidental volitional and cognitive properties be shown extrinsic by an independent, non-trivial argument that the contingent world belongs to their content-individuated truthmakers, without presupposing that extrinsic relations yield extrinsic properties?

A paper here would argue that the premise (arg1.mp) is invalid as stated, and that the modal-collapse objection can be answered only by supplying a separate, non-trivial bridge for God's volitional and cognitive properties. The paper first uses the objector's own scenario to show that grounding a relation partly in extrinsic facts does not by itself make the grounded property extrinsic, so the premise needs an independent support. It then defends a narrower claim: a volition whose correctness condition is that a particular world obtains is individuated partly by that world's identity, so the world enters the minimal truthmaker of 'God wills to create W' as the object of the act, not as a constituent of God. The argument for this runs from the intentional individuation of volitions, not from the extrinsic model. The paper answers explanatory idleness by distinguishing selection by content from intrinsic difference in the bearer: an invariant God explains which world obtains through a directed act whose content selects that world, and this selection is explanatory without intrinsic variation in God. Whether the inclusion violates simplicity is handled as a bounded question. The paper claims only that simplicity is preserved if the object is a non-constituent truthmaker, and it does not treat that as settled. Beyond the extrinsic-model defenses of Grant and Tomaszewski, the paper contributes the intentional-individuation argument for the bridge and an explicit treatment of idleness. It takes the truthmaker formulation in c013 as given and does not claim novelty for it. Its success depends on intentional individuation, which a modal-collapse proponent can contest, and the paper states that dependence openly.

**How the paper answers the main objection:** The referee's charge is that the world's inclusion in the truthmaker is either analytic or assumed. The revised paper does not stipulate inclusion. It argues for it from the individuation of volitions by content: a volition that God wills W is correct just in case W obtains, so its identity conditions involve W, and W therefore belongs to the minimal truthmaker of the volitional proposition. This is not analytic, because the claim that the truthmaker of a content-individuated attitude includes its object is substantive and a rival can deny it; the paper argues against that rival. On explanatory idleness, the paper separates selection by content from intrinsic difference in the bearer. The invariant divine act explains which world obtains by being directed at that world, and the demand that something in God settle the outcome conflates explaining an outcome with varying the explanans intrinsically. The paper concedes that this reply depends on intentional individuation and does not claim the bridge is neutral.

**Re-assessment** (fresh read, the assessor did not see its earlier critique): **not yet defensible** · overall 3.0/5 · coherence 3/5 · robustness 2/5 · significance 3/5 · specificity 4/5

*The project is a reasonable repair job with a clear plan, but its central argument conflates what individuates a volition with what makes it correct, and a first competent reader would press exactly that point.*

- Strongest remaining objection: The core argument confuses correctness conditions with individuation conditions. A volition or belief directed at W is individuated by its content: a proposition or possible world, which exists necessarily. It is not individuated by W's obtaining, just as a false belief that p is still the belief that p. Content individuation therefore puts at most a necessary abstract object into the truthmaker, not the contingent obtaining of W, and so cannot deliver extrinsicality in the sense c013 needs. Getting the contingent world into the truthmaker requires a success- or effectiveness-entailing reading of 'wills to create W'. That is just the Grant/Tomaszewski extrinsic model the paper claims to go beyond. The idleness reply inherits a dilemma. If the act's content is invariant across worlds, it cannot select which world obtains. If the content varies, then either something about God varies, which is intrinsic accident, or the content is extrinsic, which assumes the conclusion.
- What it still needs: It must give a non-question-begging argument that the minimal truthmaker of 'God wills to create W' includes W's obtaining, not merely W's content, and do so without sliding into a factive or success reading that collapses into existing extrinsic models. It also needs to show that an invariant act can select among worlds without its content varying across worlds.
